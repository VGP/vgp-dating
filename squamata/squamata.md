# Squamata calibrations

Squamates have a high density of well-preserved fossils that have been included in total-evidence phylogenetic analyses, reviewed in [Simões and Pyron 2021](https://bioone.org/journals/bulletin-of-the-museum-of-comparative-zoology/volume-163/issue-2/0027-4100-163.2.47/THE-SQUAMATE-TREE-OF-LIFE/10.3099/0027-4100-163.2.47.short). These results have been relatively consistent across most recent studies, providing a plethora of potential Type IV calibrations. In contrast, our limited sampling of squamates limits this applicability somewhat. Here, we rely on the most recent total-evidence analysis of [Simões et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/advance-article/doi/10.1093/sysbio/syaf067/8266822) and references therein, using the nomenclature of [Burbrink et al. 2020](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/69/3/502/5573126) for node names. We place the maximum age of the Triassic (252.0Ma) as the maximum age of most constraints, consistent the earliest age for stem-group Squamata subtending _Megachirella_.

Node number $XXX: Unidentata: 148.0–252.0Ma. This calibration is based on the earliest stem Scincoidea taxon _Eoscincus parvus_ (Paramacellodidae), from the Tithonian of the late Jurassic (143.1–149.2Ma).
