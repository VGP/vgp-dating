# Tetrapod calibrations

A secondary source of Type III calibrations is Title et al. 2024 (their Table S1) and references therein. We adapt those here along with a few other relevant taxa to date early tetrapods.

### Type III Calibrations:

**Node 590:** Tetrapoda | 351-337Ma - from [Carlisle et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adp7161)\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.\
**Fossil taxon:** _Lethiscus stocki_ (MCZ 2185, Museum of Comparative Zoology, Harvard).\
**Minimum age:** _Lethiscus_ has been found in the Wardie Shales of the Wardie shore, near Edinburgh, Scotland. The Wardie Shales have been assigned to the Holkerian regional stage based on fossil fishes and palynomorphs, resulting in an age of 343.5-337 Ma ([Gradstein et al. 2012](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-geologic-time-scale-2012/gradstein/978-0-444-59425-9)). This gives a minimum age estimate of 337 Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** _Lethiscus_ is either a Lepospondyli (within Batrachomorpha) or a Reptilimorpha, but since both are crown Tetrapoda it can be safely interpreted as a crown tetrapod ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [Ruta et al. 2003](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/biological-reviews/article/early-tetrapod-relationships-revisited/EF6EE684156F334264F0A58CBB40D415)).\
**Maximum age:** Whatcheeriids from the Ivorian regional Western European stage are dated to 351-346.5 Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** Whatcheeriids such as _Whatcheeria_ and _Pederpes_ are stem tetrapods, phylogenetically distant from the crown. They therefore demonstrate adequate fossilisation potential, with no evidence of crown tetrapods found at the same time.\
**Discussion:** Both baphetids and colosteids are nearer to the crown (but still stem) of Tetrapoda, and could therefore be better maximum calibrations. However, they are both younger than _Lethiscus_ ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)).

**Node 591:** Amniota | 335-315Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing lizards, turtles, birds and mammals.\
**Fossil taxon:** _Hylonomus lyelli_ (NHMUK R4168, Natural History Museum, London).\
**Minimum age:** Bashkirian, Early Pennsylvanian, Carboniferous, at 315 Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** _Hylonomus_ was recovered from division 4, section XV, coal group 15 of the Joggins Formation of Nova Scotia, Canada ([Carroll and Baird 1972](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/part/16917)). There is no absolute dating for this unit; Chronostratigraphically at Westphalian A, Carboniferous ([Calder 1998](https://www.lyellcollection.org/doi/abs/10.1144/GSL.SP.1998.143.01.19)), equivalent to late Bashkirian (~315.2-318 Ma), early Pennsylvanian, Carboniferous ([Ogg et al. 2008](https://www.cambridge.org/us/universitypress/subjects/earth-and-environmental-science/sedimentology-and-stratigraphy/concise-geologic-time-scale)). Despite considerable uncertainty regarding the placement of _Hylonomus,_ and recent debates on the composition of early amniotes ([Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [Ford and Benson 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-019-1047-3), [Jenkins et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/sysbio/syaf087/8379424), [Jenkins et al. 2025](https://peercommunityjournal.org/articles/10.24072/pcjournal.620/), [Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)), its identification as early amniote (most likely stem sauropsid) remains relatively unambiguous and forms the basis for most minimum age calibrations for crown Amniota (e.g., [Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)).
**Maximum age:** Visean, Middle Mississippian, Carboniferous, at 355 Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** The maximum age for crown amniotes (under a node-based definition: the split between synapsids and sauropsids) has been suggested to be ~332Ma, based on the age of the oldest known putative stem amniote fossils, _Westlothiana_ ([Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [van Tuinen and Hadly 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00239-004-2624-9)). Tip dating studies based on different data sets also find median ages between 320-330 Ma for crown amniotes ([Ford and Benson 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-019-1047-3), [Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)), and 335 Ma for Total Group Amniota ([Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)).


**592:** Sauria. Based on the stem archosauromorph _Protorosaurus speneri_ from the Lopingian of the Permian, 255.9-295.9Ma.

**594:** Archosauria. Based on the stem pseudosuchian _Xilousuchus sapingensis_ from the Olenekian of the Triassic, 247.2-256.0Ma.
