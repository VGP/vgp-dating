# Tetrapod calibrations

We primarily focus on the calibrations discussed by [Carlisle et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adp7161). A secondary source of calibrations is [Title et al. (2024)](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.adh2449) (their Table S1) and references therein. We adapt those here along with a few other relevant taxa to date early tetrapods.

### Calibrations:

**Node 590:** Tetrapoda | 351-337Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.\
**Fossil taxon:** _Lethiscus stocki_ (MCZ 2185, Museum of Comparative Zoology, Harvard).\
**Minimum age:** _Lethiscus_ has been found in the Wardie Shales of the Wardie shore, near Edinburgh, Scotland. The Wardie Shales have been assigned to the Holkerian regional stage based on fossil fishes and palynomorphs, resulting in an age of 343.5-337Ma ([Gradstein et al. 2012](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-geologic-time-scale-2012/gradstein/978-0-444-59425-9)). This gives a minimum age estimate of 337Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** _Lethiscus_ is either a Lepospondyli (within Batrachomorpha) or a Reptilimorpha, but since both are crown Tetrapoda it can be safely interpreted as a crown tetrapod ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [Ruta et al. 2003](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/biological-reviews/article/early-tetrapod-relationships-revisited/EF6EE684156F334264F0A58CBB40D415)).\
**Maximum age:** Whatcheeriids from the Ivorian regional Western European stage are dated to 351-346.5Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** Whatcheeriids such as _Whatcheeria_ and _Pederpes_ are stem tetrapods, phylogenetically distant from the crown. They therefore demonstrate adequate fossilisation potential, with no evidence of crown tetrapods found at the same time.\
**Discussion:** Both baphetids and colosteids are nearer to the crown (but still stem) of Tetrapoda, and could therefore be better maximum calibrations. However, they are both younger than _Lethiscus_ ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)).

**Node 591:** Amniota | 335-315Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing lizards, turtles, birds and mammals.\
**Fossil taxon:** _Hylonomus lyelli_ (NHMUK R4168, Natural History Museum, London).\
**Minimum age:** Bashkirian, Early Pennsylvanian, Carboniferous, at 315Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** _Hylonomus_ was recovered from division 4, section XV, coal group 15 of the Joggins Formation of Nova Scotia, Canada ([Carroll and Baird 1972](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/part/16917)). There is no absolute dating for this unit; Chronostratigraphically at Westphalian A, Carboniferous ([Calder 1998](https://www.lyellcollection.org/doi/abs/10.1144/GSL.SP.1998.143.01.19)), equivalent to late Bashkirian (~315.2-318Ma), early Pennsylvanian, Carboniferous ([Ogg et al. 2008](https://www.cambridge.org/us/universitypress/subjects/earth-and-environmental-science/sedimentology-and-stratigraphy/concise-geologic-time-scale)). Despite considerable uncertainty regarding the placement of _Hylonomus,_ and recent debates on the composition of early amniotes ([Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [Ford and Benson 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-019-1047-3), [Jenkins et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/sysbio/syaf087/8379424), [Jenkins et al. 2025](https://peercommunityjournal.org/articles/10.24072/pcjournal.620/), [Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)), its identification as early amniote (most likely stem sauropsid) remains relatively unambiguous and forms the basis for most minimum age calibrations for crown Amniota (e.g., [Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)).\
**Maximum age:** Visean, Middle Mississippian, Carboniferous, at 355Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** The maximum age for crown amniotes (under a node-based definition: the split between synapsids and sauropsids) has been suggested to be ~332Ma, based on the age of the oldest known putative stem amniote fossils, _Westlothiana_ ([Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [van Tuinen and Hadly 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00239-004-2624-9)). Tip dating studies based on different data sets also find median ages between 320-330Ma for crown amniotes ([Ford and Benson 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-019-1047-3), [Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)), and 335Ma for Total Group Amniota ([Simões et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/sciadv.abq1898)).

**592:** Sauria | 255.9-295.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing all extant lizards (Squamata) and crocodilians (Crocodylia).\
**Fossil taxon:** _Protorosaurus speneri_ (NMK S 180)\
**Minimum age:** 255.9Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Based on the oldest stem archosauromorph from the Lopingian (251.9–259.5Ma) of the Permian of Germany and England ([Ezcurra et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0089165)).\
**Maximum age:** 295.9Ma\
**Maximum justification:** The maximum age calibration is based on a proposed hard upper bound for the crocodile-lizard split constrained by _Apsisaurus wittei_ from the Archer City Formation of Texas ([Benton and Donoghue 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msl150)), dated to the Asselian (298.9–295.5Ma) of the early Permian.

**594:** Archosauria | 247.2-256.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing all extant birds (Aves) and crocodilians (Crocodylia).\
**Fossil taxon:** _Xilousuchus sapingensis_ (IVPP V 6026)\
**Minimum age:** 247.2Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Based on the stem pseudosuchian from the Triassic Olenekian (246.7–249.9Ma) Heshanggou Formation, Hazhen Commune, Fugu County, northeast Shaanxi Province of China ([Nesbitt 2011](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/earth-and-environmental-science-transactions-of-royal-society-of-edinburgh/article/sailbacked-suchian-from-the-heshanggou-formation-early-triassic-olenekian-of-china/E3644E8FF8F923D86FA08813311B49E5), [Parham et al. 2012](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/61/2/346/1647963)).\
**Maximum age:** 256.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** The maximum age calibration is based on a proposed soft upper bound for the bird-crocodile split constrained by the oldest putative stem archosauriform _Archosaurus rossicus_ ([Gower and Sennikov 2000](https://www.cambridge.org/us/universitypress/subjects/earth-and-environmental-science/palaeontology-and-life-history/age-dinosaurs-russia-and-mongolia)) from the late Permian Changhsingian (252.3–256.0Ma) of Russia ([Parham et al. 2012](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/61/2/346/1647963)).
