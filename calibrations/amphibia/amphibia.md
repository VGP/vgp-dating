# Amphibia calibrations

There are relatively few amphibians sampled in our tree, and relatively few amphibian fossils that have been robustly estimated in total-evidence analyses. Consequently, we rely primarily on a few well-constrained fossil taxa that appear to share robustly diagnostic synapomorphies with major crown groups. Reassuringly, molecular divergence-time estimates for lissamphibians have been relatively consistent across most recent studies, such that variability in estimated ages is not a major area of uncertainty for this group. In particular, we refer to the results of [Feng et al. (2017)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.1704632114) for frogs, [Hime et al. (2021)](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/70/1/49/5828236) for all amphibians, and [Stewart and Wiens (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1055790324002641) for salamanders as the most recent comprehensive studies with detailed discussion of fossil calibrations and age estimates. 

### Calibrations:


**Node 948:** Lissamphibia | 274.4–358.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing caecilians (Gymnophiona), frogs (Anura), and salamanders (Urodela).\
**Fossil taxon:** _Gerobatrachus hottoni_ (USNM 489135)\
**Minimum age:** 274.4Ma\
**Minimum justification:** From the Leonardian (~270–280Ma) of the early Permian, locality number USNM 40971: ‘Don's Dump Fish Quarry’ in the Clear Fork Group from Baylor County, Texas, USGS Soap Creek 7.5' quad ([Anderson et al. 2008](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06865)). The age of this formation is roughly contemporaneous with the Kungurian (274.4–283.3Ma; [Lucas et al. 2022](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357993752_Middle_Kungurian_Early_Permian_seaways_in_the_southwestern_USA)). _Gerobatrachus_ exhibits a number of features suggesting that it is a stem batrachian ([Anderson et al. 2008](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06865)), while other recent analyses suggest it is a stem lissamphibian and caution its use to calibrate crown Lissamphibia ([Kligman et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05646-5)). However, much of this uncertainty relates to a separate debate over the fossil ancestors of Lisssamphibia (Lepospondyli or Temnospondyli), which does not necessarily affect constraints for the extant lineages. Either way, essentially all recent estimates for Lissamphibia predate this taxon, including those that did not use it as a calibration. Consequently, we follow [Anderson et al. (2008)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06865) in treating it as a stem batrachian, providing a credible minimum bound for the age of Lissamphibia.\
**Maximum age:** 358.9Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** In contrast, we remain more agnostic regarding the maximum bound. Molecular estimates range from the early Permian to the late Devonian (270.9–367.0Ma; [Kligman et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05646-5)). We place a tentative soft maximum age of 358.9Ma on this node, corresponding to the Devonian–Carboniferous boundary.\
**Discussion:** [Anderson et al. (2008)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06865) suggested a minimum age of 328–335Ma for the age of crown Lissamphibia based on the earliest appearance of lepospondyls and temnospondyls together. However, recent molecular estimates such as [Feng et al. (2017)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1704632114), [Hime et al. (2021)](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/70/1/49/5828236), and [Stewart and Wiens (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1055790324002641) – potentially misreading [Anderson (2008)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11692-008-9044-5) – confusingly placed a maximum age of 337Ma on this node. [Kligman et al. (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05646-5) – despite disagreeing with Anderson et al. (2008) regarding the fossil ancestors of Lissamphibia – nevertheless concur in supporting a Mississippian origin (323.4–358.9Ma) of Lissamphibia. We take a more conservative approach in setting a minimum age based on the oldest putative stem batrachian Gerobatrachus, with a maximum that modestly predates the middle-Carboniferous consensus age of the Mississippian ([Kligman et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05646-5)). This range encompasses essentially all previous molecular divergence-time estimates, regardless of calibration strategy.

**Node 949:** Batrachia | 250.0–272.8Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing frogs (Anura), and salamanders (Urodela).\
**Fossil taxon:** _Triadobatrachus massinoti_ (MNHN.F.MAE 126)\
**Minimum age:** 250.0Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The earliest unambiguous stem anuran based on a number of synapomorphies ([Ruta and Coates 2007](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1017/S1477201906002008)), from the base of the middle part of the Sakamena Group in northern Madagascar dated imprecisely to the earliest Triassic for which we use 250.0Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)).\
**Maximum age:** 272.8Ma\
**Maximum justification:** [Benton et al. (2015)](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1) suggested a soft maximum age in the middle Permian, which agrees with the recommendation of [Anderson et al. (2008)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06865) based on _Gerobatrachus_ as a stem batrachian.\
**Discussion:** This taxon was strongly supported as a stem anuran by the total-evidence dating analysis of [Pyron (2011)](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/60/4/466/1611723), reinforcing its use as a minimum bound on the age of Batrachia.

**Node 950:** Anura | 165.3–201.5Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing all extant frogs.\
**Fossil taxon:** _Eodiscoglossus oxoniensis_ (NHMUK R11700)\
**Minimum age:** 165.3Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The earliest unambiguous crown anuran based on a number of synapomorphies ([Evans et al. 1990](https://palass.org/sites/default/files/media/publications/palaeontology/volume_33/vol33_part2_pp299-311.pdf), [Ruta and Coates 2007](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1017/S1477201906002008)), from the middle Jurassic Forest Marble Formation, upper Bathonian of Oxfordshire, England ([Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)). \
**Maximum age:** 201.5Ma\
**Maximum justification:** [Benton et al. (2015)](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1) suggested a soft maximum age at the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary, given other stem anurans and small tetrapods from numerous deposits at that time without any apparent crown anurans.\
**Discussion:** The total-evidence analysis of [Chen et al. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep19209) estimated the congeneric _Eodiscoglossus santonjae_ in the crown of Anura, corroborated by the morphological re-description by [Baez and Gomez 2016](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/60180) and re-analysis by [Gao and Chen 2017](https://bioone.org/journals/American-Museum-Novitates/volume-2017/issue-3876/3876.1/A-New-Crown-Group-Frog-Amphibia--Anura-from-the/10.1206/3876.1.short). This taxon dates to the Barremian (121.4–125.8Ma) of the early Cretaceous La Pedrera de Rúbies Formation and El Castellar Formation of Spain. Other putative crown-group anurans are known from similar ages as summarized in [Gomez and Andres 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1631068319301320), but these are not as well-documented.

**Node 970:** Dorsipares | 148.1–201.5Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing _Rhinophrynus_ (Rhinophrynidae) and _Xenopus_ (Pipidae).\
**Fossil taxon:** _Rhadinosteus parvus_ (DINO 14693)\
**Minimum age:** 148.1Ma\
**Minimum justification:** From the Brushy Basin Member of the Morrison Formation, from the Kimmeridgian of the late Jurassic, in Dinosaur National Monument, Utah, USA. The sister group to _Rhinophrynus_ (Rhinophrynidae) based on a number of synapomorphies ([Henrici 1998](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02724634.1998.10011060)) and parsimony-based morphological phylogenetic analyses by [Báez (2013)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0195667112001607), [Agnolin et al. (2020)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0895981120301462), and the supertree analysis of [Perez-Ben et al. (2024)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/paleobiology/article/frog-limbs-in-deep-time-is-jumping-locomotion-at-the-roots-of-the-anuran-bauplan/AC8EC495E05D51667CAAE5B18FAD1E67).\
**Maximum age:** 201.5Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Given the similarity in ages, we follow [Benton et al. (2015)](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1) for crown Anura in placing a soft maximum age at the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary, given other stem anurans and small tetrapods from numerous deposits at that time without any apparent crown anurans.\
**Discussion:** The proper name for this clade is Dorsipares ([Dubois et al. 2021](https://www.mapress.com/mt/article/view/38637)), rather than Pipoidea as referred to by most authors ([Cannatella 2015](https://karger.com/cgr/article-abstract/145/3-4/283/62763)).\




### Likelihood checkpoints:

There are well-documented times for likelihood checkpoints at the base of Amphibia. Previous estimates for Lissamphibia (Gymnophiona + Batrachia) with and without _Gerobatrachus_ (depending on its placement as a stem batrachian) were previously compiled by [Kligman et al. (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05646-5). We summarize those here to obtain a 95% CI from the quantiles. Those authors also summarized the published evidence for Batrachia (Salientia + Urodela) and Gymnophiona (Rhinatrematidae + Stegokrotaphia*), which we analyzed in the same manner:

**948:** Lissamphibia with _Gerobatrachus_: 278–361Ma (Median 320Ma)\
**948:** Lissamphibia without _Gerobatrachus_: 316–363Ma (Median 326Ma)\
**949:** Batrachia: 219–348Ma (Median 292Ma)\
**977:** Gymnophiona: 91.6–259Ma (Median 216Ma)
