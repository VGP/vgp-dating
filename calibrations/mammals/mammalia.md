# Mammal calibrations

Our calibrations derive primarily from Upham et al. ([2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000494)) and Álvarez-Carretero et al. ([2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04341-1)), with recent updates and revisions therein. Many of the calibrations were originally derived from Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)) and Phillips ([2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)), which we indicate when appropriate. Minimum ages are hard cutoffs, while maximum ages are ‘soft maxima’ where the upper 95% of the age distribution is younger than the given age and the remaining 5% tapers off in accordance with the prior’s shape (e.g., skew–_t_; [Álvarez-Carretero et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04341-1)).


## Calibrations:

**Node 783:** Mammalia | 164.9–201.5Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Most recent common ancestor of monotremes (_Ornithorhynchus anatinus_), marsupials (_Monodelphis domestica_), and placentals (_Dasypus novemcinctus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Ambondro mahabo_ (UA 10602) from the Isalo III Formation of the Mahajanga Basin in northwestern Madagascar ([Flynn et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/43420))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Jurassic (Bathonian), 164.9Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Specimen locality dated to Bathonian, top of which is 166.1Ma, ± 1.2 Myr = 164.9Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))\
**Maximum age:** Jurassic-Triassic Boundary, 201.5Ma\
**Maximum justification:** The closest known relative of crown mammals, †_Hadrocodium_, dates to early Jurassic Sinemurian ([Luo et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1058476)), and other closely related mammaliaformes are known from the Late Triassic ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known member of the monotreme clade Australosphenida from the middle Jurassic (Bathonian) of Madagascar ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424), [Flynn et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/43420), [Luo et al. 2002](https://www.app.pan.pl/article/pdf/41/item/app47-001.html), [Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291)), and the oldest-known credible crown mammalian.

---

**Node 784:** Theria | 156.3–169.6Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of marsupials (_Monodelphis domestica_) and placentals (_Dasypus novemcinctus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Juramaia sinensis_ (PM1143) from the Daxigou site in the Middle–Late Jurassic Tiaojishan formation, Jianchang County, Liaoning Province, China ([Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291))\
**Minimum age:** Late Jurassic (Oxfordian), 156.3Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Radiometric dating sets the age of the Beipiao area at the Daxigou site at just over 160Ma, placing it within the Late Jurassic (Oxfordian), with a minimum bound of 157.3Ma ± 1.0 Myr, thus 156.3Ma ([Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291))\
**Maximum age:** Middle Jurassic (Bathonian), 169.6Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Following the rationale of Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)), we set the maximum age at 169.6Ma to account for viewpoints that place taxa such as †_Ambondro_ (see **Node 783:** Mammalia) as stem therians ([Woodburne et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1055-7903(03)00113-1)), constraining the therian maximum within the Jurassic (Bathonian) at 168.6Ma ± 1.0 Myr, thus 169.6Ma.\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem eutherian ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424), [Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291)), and also the oldest-known crown therian.

---

**Node 947:** Monotremata | 16.0–113.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Most recent common ancestor of Tachyglossidae (_Tachyglossus aculeatus_) and Ornithorhynchidae (_Ornithorhynchus anatinus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Obdurodon dicksoni_ (QMF20568) from the Riversleigh World Heritage Area in Queensland, Australia ([Archer et al. 1992](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/608888))\
**Minimum age:** Early Miocene, 15.97Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Oldest specimens found in northwestern Queensland in Faunal Zone B sites, which date to the early Miocene ([Travouillon et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1080/03115510609506871))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous, 113.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Potential crown Monotremes are present in Argentina from ~61Ma, but the latest Australasian sites that lack any evidence of crown monotremes date to the Albian of the early Cretaceous ([Phillips 2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490))\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem member of Ornithorhynchidae from the early Miocene (Burdigalian) of northwestern Queensland, Australia ([Phillips 2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490), [Luo et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05627), [Phillips et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0904649106)), and the oldest-known crown monotrematan.

---

**Node 938:** Marsupialia | 47.6–131.3Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Most recent common ancestor of Paucituberculata (_Lestoros inca_), Didelphimorphia (_Monodelphis domestica_) and Australidelphia (_Dromiciops gliroides_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Djarthia murgonensis_ (QMF52748) from the Eocene Tingamarra Local Fauna at the Murgon fossil site in southeastern Queensland, Australia ([Godthelp et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1023%2FA%3A1020517808869))\
**Minimum age:** Early Eocene (Ypresian), 47.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Murgon locality is correlated with the Ypresian marine stage ([Beck 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-012-0953-x))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian), 131.3Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Therians such as †_Sinodelphys szalayi_ date to the Barremian ([Luo et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1090718), [Zhou et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01420)) and are diverse, but they are evidently not metatherians ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3), [Beck 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-08419-5_35)). The diversity of therians and possible eutherians during the Barremian implies a similar antiquity for Metatheria to at least that time, but such taxa pre-date the radiation of crown Marsupialia.\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known crown marsupial, from the early Eocene of Murgon, Australia ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424), Beck [2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-012-0953-x), [2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-08419-5_35)).

**Node 785:** Placentalia | 61.6–125.8Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Atlantogenata (_Dasypus novemcinctus_) and Boroeutheria (_Tupaia tana_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Ravenictis krausei_ (UALVP 31175) from the Ravenscrag Formation of the early Puercan of Saskatchewan, Canada ([Fox and Youzwyshyn 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.1994.10011566))\
**Minimum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 61.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Ravenscrag Formation is from the early Puercan NALMA, corresponding to the Danian age of the Paleocene, the top of which corresponds to 61.7Ma ± 0.1 Myr, thus 61.6Ma ([Fox and Youzwyshyn 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.1994.10011566))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian/Aptian), 125.77Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Maximum bound based on the Jehol Formation of the Early Cretaceous ([Zhou 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwu055), [Yang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1918272117)), a well-sampled fauna including stem placentals such as †_Ambolestes_ and †_Sinodelphys_ ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3)) but no putative crown members. A soft-maximum in the Jurassic for crown placental clades (cf. [Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)) is arguably more conservative, as such soft constraints do not penalize truly ancient crown placental divergences as much. However, given methodological difficulties arising from broad prior distributions that can artifactually favor older ages ([Budd and Mann 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syad057)), we instead use tighter soft maxima derived from the diversity of lower Cretaceous mammalian faunae (e.g., from the Jehol biota), which document a variety of therian species but no crown placentals. Given the nature of "soft" maxima, divergence estimates older than 126Ma are still possible, but 126Ma better reflects the negative information present in Lower Cretaceous localities. In the case of crown placentals, the fossil record suggests that, at this time, absence of evidence really does indicate evidence of absence.\
**Discussion:** This fossil is a stem carnivoran from the early Paleocene (Danian) of Ravenscrag Formation in Saskatchewan, Canada ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424), [Fox et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1666/09-165.1)), and the oldest-known crown placentalian.

**Node 931:** Afrotheria | 59.2–125.8Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Paenungulata (_Dugong dugon_) and Afroinsectiphilia (_Rhynchocyon petersi_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Ocepeia daouiensis_ (CPSGM MA1) from the Ouled Abdoun Basin (Paleocene), Morocco ([Gheerbrant et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089739))\
**Minimum age:** Late Paleocene (Selandian), 59.24Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Gheerbrant et al. ([2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089739)) specify a Selandian age for this taxon, which is evidently the same locality as †_Eritherium azzouzorum_, previously described as "early Thanetian (incl. Selandian)" ([Gheerbrant 2009](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0900251106)). Here, we follow Gheerbrant et al. ([2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089739)) who write "...The new material of this species confirms its Paleocene age. It comes from the same bone bed level located at the base of phosphorite Bed IIa that is dated Selandian … and that yields _Eritherium azzouzorum_, _Abdounodus hamdii_, and _Lahimia selloumi_."\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian/Aptian), 125.77Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Maximum bound based on the Jehol Formation of the Early Cretaceous ([Zhou 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwu055), [Yang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1918272117)), a well-sampled fauna including stem placentals such as †_Ambolestes_ and †_Sinodelphys_ ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3)) but no putative crown members\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem proboscidean from the Paleocene (Selandian) of Morocco ([Gheerbrant et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089739)), and the oldest-known crown afrotherian.

**Node 936:** Xenarthra | 47.6–125.8Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Cingulata (_Dasypus novemcinctus_) and Folivora (_Choloepus didactylus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Riostegotherium yanei_ (MCN-PV 1774) from the Itaboraí Formation of the Itaboraian land-mammal age (middle Paleocene) of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil ([Oliveira and Bergqvist 1998](https://peapaleontologica.org.ar/index.php/peapa/article/view/174))\
**Minimum age:** Early Eocene (Ypresian), 47.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Itaboraí formation dates to the Ypresian of the early Eocene ([Woodburne et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10914-012-9222-1)), the top of which is 47.8Ma, ± 0.2 Myr, thus 47.6Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian/Aptian), 125.77Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Maximum bound based on the Jehol Formation of the Early Cretaceous ([Zhou 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwu055), [Yang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1918272117)), a well-sampled fauna including stem placentals such as †_Ambolestes_ and †_Sinodelphys_ ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3)) but no putative crown members\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem cingulate from the early Eocene (Ypresian) of Itaboraí, Brazil ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424), [Bergqvist et al. 2004](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/en/periodiques/geodiversitas/26/2/les-xenarthra-mammalia-du-bassin-de-sao-jose-de-itaborai-paleocene-superieur-age-itaboraiense-rio-de-janeiro-bresil)), and the oldest-known crown xenarthran.

**Node 845:** Chiroptera | 47.9–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Yinpterochiroptera (_Rousettus aegyptiacus_) and Yangochiroptera (_Rhynchonycteris naso_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Tachypteron franzeni_ (Institut Royal des Sciences Naturelles de Belgique, Bruxelles, specimen BE 4-119a+b) from the Grube Messel oil shale, near Darmstadt, Germany ([Storch et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02989856))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Eocene (Lutetian: MP11), 47.9Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Recent data "based on the short eccentricity amplitude modulations of the regional pollen rain and their correlation to the astronomical La2010a/La2010d solutions in combination with a revised 40Ar/39Ar age of a basalt fragment from a lapilli tuff section below the first lacustrine sediments" update the Messel eruption and the MP11 stage to the Ypresian/Lutetian boundary with a minimum age of 48.11Ma ± 0.22 ([Lenz et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00531-014-1126-2))\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Phillips ([2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)) suggested using the base of the Thanetian (late Paleocene: 58.9Ma) as the upper bound to be conservative regarding numerous stem bats that are from very close to the Thanetian-Ypresian boundary ([Ravel et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-011-0785-0)). To harmonize our maximum age for bats with other crown placentals, we extend this to the base of the Danian (early Paleocene: 66.0Ma). Phillips [2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490) reported that removing the maximum age for Chiroptera resulted in a Selandian age estimate, ~60.2Ma.\
**Discussion:** Jones et al. ([2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10914-024-09705-8)) identify the Lutetian †_Tachypteron franzeni_ as the oldest-known definitive crown chiropteran and the Ypresian †_Archaeonycteris praecursor_ as the oldest stem taxon based on morphological phylogenetic analysis. They recover †_T. franzeni_ on the stem of Miniopteridae (Vespertilionoidea) in Yangochiroptera, but we avoid this level of crown-group precision pending an updated total-evidence analysis. The original description placed it in total-group Emballonuridae ([Storch et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02989856)). Jones et al. ([2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10914-024-09705-8)) also estimate †_Microchiropteryx foliae_ as a stem vespertilionoid in crown Yangochiroptera, but other recent analyses have recovered this taxon as a stem bat ([Hand et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.09.043)). Another candidate is †_Tanzanycteris mannardi_ (TNM MP-207) from the middle Eocene lake sediments of the Mahenge formation, Tanzania ([Gunnell et al. 2003](https://palaeo-electronica.org/2002_2/africa/issue2_02.htm)). This fossil has been considered the oldest-known stem member of Rhinolophoidea within Yinpterochiroptera, and therefore the oldest-known crown chiropteran ([Phillips 2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490), [Flynn et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/43420), [Gunnell et al. 2003](https://palaeo-electronica.org/2002_2/africa/issue2_02.htm)). Age estimates using 206Pb/238U dating and sedimentary rate analysis put the lacustrine Mahenge formation at 45.83Ma ± 1.1 Myr and the fossil itself at a lower bound of 45.0Ma ([Harrison et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4615-1271-4_2)).

**Node 876:** Yinpterochiroptera | 33.9–58.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Pteropodidae (_Rousettus aegyptiacus_) and Rhinolophidae (_Rhinolophus trifoliatus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Saharaderma pseudovampyrus_ (CGM 8367) from quarry L-41, lower sequence of the Jebel Qatrani formation, Fayûm depression, Egypt ([Gunnell et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1671/0272-4634(2008)28[1:NBMCFT]2.0.CO;2))\
**Minimum age:** Latest Eocene (Priabonian), 33.9Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Fayûm Quarry L-41 where †_Saharaderma_ was discovered has been dated to the Priabonian, with an upper bound of 33.9Ma ([Seiffert 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0600689103))\
**Maximum age:** Late Paleocene (Thanetian), 58.9Ma\
**Maximum justification:** We adapt the suggestion of Phillips ([2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)), using the base of the Thanetian (late Paleocene: 58.9Ma) as the upper bound to be conservative regarding numerous stem bats from the Thanetian-Ypresian boundary ([Ravel et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-011-0785-0))\
**Discussion:** Jones et al. ([2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10914-024-09705-8)) identify the Priabonian †_Saharaderma pseudovampyrus_ as a stem member of Megadermatidae, and therefore the oldest-known definitive crown yinpterochiropteran. To our knowledge, no other placements have been suggested based on phylogenetic analysis. Other early possible yinpterochiropterans are more uncertain, such as †_Qarunycteris moerisae_ from Egypt ([Gunnell et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1671/0272-4634(2008)28[1:NBMCFT]2.0.CO;2)) and †_Brachipposideros tedfordi_ from Australia ([Hand 1997](https://search.informit.org/doi/10.3316/informit.T2025082200008990688579640)), which are younger (Oligocene) and more difficult to constrain adequately. The first convincing pteropodid fossils do not show up until the mid-Miocene (see discussion in [Almeida et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13127-021-00480-5)).

**Node 847:** Vespertilionoidea | 37.8–58.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Molossidae (_Molossus nigricans_) and Miniopteridae (_Miniopterus schreibersii_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Cuvierimops parisiensis_ (LEB1_CupprA.1.7, left M1/ [Plate 2f], from the collections of UM2) from the late Eocene Quercy phosphorites France ([Maitre 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13358-014-0069-3))\
**Minimum age:** Late Eocene (Priabonian), 37.8Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The earliest †_Cuvierimops parisiensis priscus_ material is from the Lébratières 1 site (MP 17a) in the Phosphorites du Quercy, which occur below a layer dated to 37.97Ma ± 0.22 ([Maitre 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13358-014-0069-3))\
**Maximum age:** Late Paleocene (Thanetian), 58.9Ma\
**Maximum justification:** We adapt the suggestion of Phillips ([2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)), using the base of the Thanetian (late Paleocene: 58.9Ma) as the upper bound to be conservative regarding numerous stem bats from the Thanetian-Ypresian boundary ([Ravel et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-011-0785-0))\
**Discussion:** Jones et al. ([2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10914-024-09705-8)) identify the Priabonian †_Cuvierimops_ as a stem member of Molossidae, and therefore the oldest-known definitive crown member of Vespertilionoidea. Other material, including postcrania, strengthens this placement ([Losco and Martin 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12542-025-00746-9)).

**Node 869:** Stem Noctilionoidea | 26.0–58.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Emballonuroidea (_Rhynchonycteris naso_: Emballonuridae) and Noctilionoidea (_Phyllostomus discolor_: Phyllostomidae)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Koopmanycteris palaeomormoops_ (UF 157765) from Brooksville 2 LF, early Arikareean NALMA, Hernando County, Florida, USA ([Morgan et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1206/0003-0090.434.1.1))\
**Minimum age:** Late Oligocene (Chattian stage: Arikareean NALMA), 26Ma\
**Minimum justification:** As noted by Morgan et al. ([2019](https://doi.org/10.1206/0003-0090.434.1.1)): "similarity between the chiropteran faunas of the Whitneyan I-75 LF and the Brooksville 2 LF supports an early Arikareean age for Brooksville, between 26 and 28Ma" (see also [Hayes 2000](https://doi.org/10.58782/flmnh.isca2513))\
**Maximum age:** Late Paleocene (Thanetian), 58.9Ma\
**Maximum justification:** We adapt the suggestion of Phillips ([2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)), using the base of the Thanetian (late Paleocene: 58.9Ma) as the upper bound to be conservative regarding numerous stem bats from the Thanetian-Ypresian boundary ([Ravel et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-011-0785-0))\
**Discussion:** As noted by Morgan et al. ([2019](https://doi.org/10.1206/0003-0090.434.1.1)), numerous characters appear to convincingly unite this taxon with Mormoopidae (Noctilionoidea), rendering it the oldest-known definitive total-group noctilionoid.

**Node 916:** Euarchonta | 65.8–125.8Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Primata (_Homo sapiens_), Dermoptera (_Cynocephalus volans_), and Scandentia (_Tupaia tana_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Purgatorius mckeeveri_ (UCMP 150021) from the Harley’s Point locality in the Tullock Member of the Fort Union Formation in Montana, USA ([Wilson Mantilla et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.210050))\
**Minimum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 65.79Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Specimen is from the Harley’s Point locality in the Tullock Member of the Fort Union Formation, Montana, and has been dated to the early Danian magnetochron C29R ([Wilson Mantilla et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.210050)), which corresponds to the early Puercan NALMA\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian/Aptian), 125.77Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Maximum bound based on the Jehol Formation of the Early Cretaceous ([Zhou 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwu055), [Yang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1918272117)), a well-sampled fauna including stem placentals such as †_Ambolestes_ and †_Sinodelphys_ ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3)) but no putative crown members\
**Discussion:** As noted by de Vries and Beck ([2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249)): "recent phylogenetic analyses specifically intended to resolve euarchontan relationships consistently place _Purgatorius_ and other “plesiadapiforms” within crown Euarchonta, and specifically closer to Primates and Dermoptera than to Scandentia, although the precise affinities of “plesiadapiforms” vary in these analyses." See Chester et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421707112)) for an example.

**Node 918:** Primates | 56.0–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Strepsirrhini (_Lemur catta_) and Haplorhini (_Homo sapiens_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Altiatlasius koulchii_ (THR 141) from the Adrar Mgorn 1 locality in the Jbel Guersif Formation of the Ouarzazate Basin, Morocco ([Sigé et al. 1990](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/pala/detail/A214/71382/iAltiatlasius_koulchii_i_n_gen_et_sp_primate_omomyide_du_Paleocene_superieur_du_Maroc_et_les_origines_des_euprimates))\
**Minimum age:** Late Paleocene (Thanetian), 56.0Ma\
**Minimum justification:** This formation is dated to the Thanetian, 57.6 ± 1.6Ma, yielding 56.0Ma (reviewed in [Dragastan and Herbig 2007](https://doi.org/10.2113/gsmicropal.53.1-2.1))\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Putative stem primates such as plesiadapids, paromomyids, and carpolestids from the Danian without any crown taxa implies a soft maximum age at the base of the Paleocene ([McKenna and Bell 1997](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/classification-of-mammals/9780231110136/), [Kondrashov et al. 2004](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281111353_REVISED_DISTRIBUTION_OF_CONDYLARTHS_MAMMALIA_EUTHERIA_IN_ASIA), [Benton et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199535033.001.0001), [Bloch et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0610579104))\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the earliest-known primate and putative stem haplorhine from the late Paleocene (Thanetian) of Morocco ([Sigé et al. 1990](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/pala/detail/A214/71382/iAltiatlasius_koulchii_i_n_gen_et_sp_primate_omomyide_du_Paleocene_superieur_du_Maroc_et_les_origines_des_euprimates); see reviews and references in [Williams et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0908320107), [Benton et al. 2015](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1), [Pattinson et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syu077), and [dos Reis et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syy001)). An alternative would be †_Purgatorius_, which is described from dental (e.g. UCMP 107406) and tarsal (e.g. UCMP 197509) remains and has a well-constrained age of 66.0–63.4Ma, but is not consistently recovered in crown Primates ([Chester et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421707112)). Within crown Primates, another similarly aged alternative is †_Teilhardina brandti_, a well-constrained euprimate (†Omomyidae) with material known from the Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum, ~56Ma ([Rose et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.21579), [Morse et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhevol.2018.08.005), [de Vries and Beck 2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249)).

**Node 929:** Strepsirrhini | 33.9–56.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Lorisoidea (_Nycticebus coucang_) and Lemuroidea (_Lemur catta_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Karanisia clarki_ (CGM40265) from the Birket Quaran Formation (Eocene: Priabonian) of Fayûm, Egypt ([Seiffert et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01489))\
**Minimum age:** Late Eocene (Priabonian), 33.9Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Birket Quaran Formation corresponds to upper Priabonian ([Seiffert 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0600689103))\
**Maximum age:** Late Paleocene (Thanetian), 56.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Bounded by the first appearance of euprimates, represented by †_Altiatlasius koulchii_ (see **Node 918:** Primates), which dates to the late Paleocene at 56.0Ma ([Sigé et al. 1990](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/pala/detail/A214/71382/iAltiatlasius_koulchii_i_n_gen_et_sp_primate_omomyide_du_Paleocene_superieur_du_Maroc_et_les_origines_des_euprimates)), [Dragastan and Herbig 2007](https://doi.org/10.2113/gsmicropal.53.1-2.1))\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem lorisiform or crown strepsirrhine (of uncertain affinities) from the late Eocene (Priabonian) Birket Quaran Formation of Fayûm, Egypt ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424), [Seiffert et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01489), [Seiffert 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0600689103))

**Node 919:** Anthropoidea | 33.9–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Catarrhini (_Homo sapiens_) and Platyrrhini (_Callithrix jacchus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Catopithecus browni_ (DPC 8701) from the Fayum Depression of Egypt ([Simons 1989](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.86.24.9956))\
**Minimum age:** Late Eocene (Priabonian), 33.9Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Fayûm Quarry L-41 where †_Catopithecus_ was discovered has been dated to the Priabonian, with an upper bound of 33.9Ma ([Seiffert 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0600689103))\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Following Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)), the Early Paleocene is set as the maximum age, as there are no definitive crown primates from the Danian and the split must predate the potential anthropoid sister-taxon †_Altiatlasius koulchii_ (see **Node 918:** Primates; [Seiffert et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1116569)) of 56.0Ma.\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known crown catarrhine from the late Eocene (Priabonian) of Fayûm, Egypt ([Seiffert 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0600689103))

**Node 920:** Catarrhini | 24.4–34.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Cercopithecoidea (_Macaca fascicularis_) and Hominoidea (_Homo sapiens_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Rukwapithecus fleaglei_ (RRBP 12444A, Ohio University USA) from the Nsungwe Formation, Rukwa Rift Basin, Tanzania ([Stevens et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12161))\
**Minimum age:** Late Oligocene (Chattian), 24.44Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Radiometric dating constrains the Nsungwe Formation to 24.93 ± 0.49Ma ([Roberts et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jafrearsci.2009.09.002))\
**Maximum age:** Early Oligocene (Rupelian), 34.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Fossils referred to the stem of Catarrhini that predate the Cercopithecoidea/Hominoidea split are found in the Fayûm beds in Egypt, with the oldest anthropoid primates dated to 33.9–28.4 ± 0.1Ma ([Seiffert et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1116569), [Seiffert 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0600689103), [Stevens et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12161))\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem hominoid and crown catarrhine, from the Nsungwe Formation, Rukwa Rift Basin, Tanzania ([Roberts et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jafrearsci.2009.09.002), [Stevens et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12161), [Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))

**Node 915:** Lagomorpha | 47.6–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Leporidae (_Oryctolagus cuniculus_) and Ochotonidae (_Ochotona princeps_).\
**Fossil taxa:** Unnamed leporid fossils (GU/RSR/VAS 711) from the early Eocene Cambay Shales at the Vastan lignite mine, Gujarat, India ([Rose et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.1661))\
**Minimum age:** Early Eocene (Ypresian), 47.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Cambay Shales are dated to the middle Ypresian ([Rose et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.1661)), the top of which dates to 47.8Ma ± 0.2 Myr, thus 47.6Ma\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Based on the occurrences of basal Glires, including †_Mimotona_ ([Asher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387), [de Vries and Beck, 2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249)) and †_Heomys_ ([Li and Ting, 1983](https://doi.org/10.5962/p.228600))\
**Discussion:** These unnamed stem leporid fossils are the oldest-known crown lagomorphs, from the Vastan Mines (Cambay Shale) of West-Central India, dated to the early Eocene (Ypresian) ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424), [Rose et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.1661)). The oldest known stem Lagomorphs (e.g., †_Heomys_) are known from the lower Paleocene ([Asher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387), [de Vries and Beck, 2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249)).

**Node 892:** Rodentia | 41.0–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Muroidea (_Mus musculus_), Sciuromorpha (_Sciurus vulgaris_), and Hystricomorpha (_Erethizon dorsatum_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Ischyromys_ sp. (USNM PAL 617532) from West Canyon Creek, Wyoming, USA ([Heaton 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.019))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Eocene (Lutetian), 41.03Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The taxon is known from Late Eocene deposits at West Canyon Creek, Wyoming, dated to the Duchesnean NALMA ([Heaton 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.019)), and Hough Draw, Montana, dated to the late Uintan (Ui3) NALMA, corresponding to the Lutetian ([Tabrum et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.015))\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Soft bound based on presence of likely stem Glires and absence of crown Glires in Lower Member of Wanghudun Formation, suggesting that the Rodentia + Lagomorphia split had not yet occurred ([de Vries and Beck, 2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249))\
**Discussion:** †_Ischyromys_ sp. is a stem member of Sciuromorpha nested well within crown Rodentia ([Asher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387)), with the oldest specimens known from the Uintan (Ui3) NALMA (Lutetian) of Montana, USA (Hough Draw, [Tabrum et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.015)). Older minimum ages for crown Rodentia would be justified if future studies sampling both extant and fossil species support the placements of taxa such as †_Tamquammys_ close to Ctenohystrica ([Marivaux and Boivin 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/zoolinnean/zlz048)) or †_Franimys_ close to Sciuridae ([Marivaux et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2004.00131.x)). Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)) advocated for using †_Paramys atavus_ (YPM-PU 14200) to calibrate crown Rodentia, citing several papers that placed †_Paramys_ in Sciuromorpha, within the crown group ([Korth 1984](https://doi.org/10.5962/p.228603), [McKenna and Bell 1997](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/classification-of-mammals/9780231110136/), [Marivaux et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2004.00131.x)). However, †“_Paramys_” _atavus_ is likely better assigned as †_Acritoparamys atavus_ ([Ivy 1990](https://hdl.handle.net/2027.42/48538)). More recent work that did not sample †_A. atavus_ but did code †_Paramys_ sp, †_P. delicatus_, and †_P. copei_, as well as †_Reithroparamys_ and older Asian Paleocene species such as †_Tribosphenomys_, found that these taxa are stem rather than crown rodents ([Meng et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1206/0003-0090(2003)275%3C0001:TOORMG%3E2.0.CO;2), [Asher et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1107808), [2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387)). While Vianey-Liaud and Marivaux ([2021](https://doi.org/10.18563/pv.44.2.e2)) reconstruct †_Acritoparamys_ as falling within crown Rodentia, the analysis includes no extant taxa. We therefore suggest †_Ischyromys_ as a younger and more conservative calibration for crown rodents at present.

**Node 914:** Hystricomorpha | 31.6–56.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Ctenodactylomorphi (_Ctenodactylus gundi_) and Hystricognathi (_Erethizon dorsatum_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Eoviscaccia frassinetti_ (SGOPV 2935) from the Tinguiririca River valley, Termas del Flaco, Chile ([Bertrand et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1206/3750.2))\
**Minimum age:** Early Oligocene (Tinguirican) 31.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The locality dates to the ?late Eocene–early Oligocene Tinguirican SALMA, with whole-rock K/Ar dates ranging in mean age from 31.3–35.6Ma (see [Bertrand et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1206/3750.2), [Dunn et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1130/B30660.1), with 31.65 ± 0.32Ma and 31.34 ± 0.17Ma in the type area)\
**Maximum age:** Late Paleocene (Thanetian), 56.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** As noted by Phillips ([2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)): "the absence of any hystricognaths from the better sampled Early Eocene faunas ([Marivaux et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/14772019.2011.562555), [Coster et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1130/B30565.1)) provides a more appropriate soft maximum of basal Ypresian age."\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem chinchilloid and crown hystricomorphan that has been strongly supported (1.0 pp) in a recent total-evidence analysis ([Carrillo et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2025.2586)). A potentially older stem caviomorph rodent, †_Cachiyacuy contamanensis_ (MUSM1871), is known from locality CTA-27 at the top of the Yahuarango Formation near Contamana, Peru, which has been dated to a minimum of 40.94Ma (Eocene: Lutetian or Bartonian) based on radioisotopy, vertebrate biochronology and palynostratigraphy ([Antoine et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2011.1732)). Phillips ([2014](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)) used this fossil to calibrate the Caviomorphia + Phiomorpha split, but it could still function to provide a lower bound for Hystricomorpha. However, Campbell et al. ([2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2105956118)) concluded that "there are currently no compelling Eocene records of either rodents or primates in the known fossil record of South America" – also see Arnal et al. ([2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/08912963.2021.2017916)). The CTA-27 locality has also been dated to the Late/Mid Eocene ([Antoine et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2011.1732)) and to the Lower Oligocene ([Campbell et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2105956118)); this conflict in dates gives us reason to disfavour †_Cachiyacuy_ for use in calibration. Total-group ctenohystricids may still have early Paleogene records elsewhere (e.g., chapattimyids, †_Tamquammys_; see [Marivaux et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2004.00131.x)), but we tentatively refrain from using such records as calibrations pending a phylogenetic analysis broadly sampled for morphology and DNA across living and fossil species.

**Node 906:** Castorimorpha | 37.7–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Geomyoidea (_Thomomys bottae_) and Castoroidea (_Castor canadensis_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Heliscomys walshi_ (LACM 153717) from the Sespe Formation of California, USA ([Kelly 2009](https://rivp-paludicola.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/7-3-kelly-2009.pdf))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Eocene (Bartonian), 37.71Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Known from the Simi Valley Landfill Local Fauna, middle Eocene (Duchesnean NALMA, 38–37.8Ma), corresponding to the top of the Bartonian (37.71Ma)\
**Maximum age:** Danian, 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Soft bound based on presence of likely stem Glires and absence of crown Glires in Lower Member of Wanghudun Formation, suggesting that the Rodentia + Lagomorphia split had not yet occurred ([de Vries and Beck, 2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249))\
**Discussion:** The oldest-known crown castorimorphan. More complete cranioskeletal material known from †_Heliscomys ostranderi_ (Chadronian) in an analysis with morphological and molecular data from fossil and living taxa supports an independent Eocene record for geomyoids ([Asher et al. 2019:fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387)), with 0.95 pp support for †_Heliscomys_ + crown geomyoids to the exclusion of _Castor_, and weaker support (0.5 pp) for an older taxon (†_Paradjidaumo_ from the Uintan NALMA) with geomyoids.

**Node 896:** Stem Murinae | 16.0–18.3Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Murinae (_Mus musculus_) and Deomyinae (_Acomys russatus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Potwarmus primitivus_ (YGSP 591) from the lower and middle Siwalik deposits in the Potwar Plateau of Pakistan ([Wessels et al. 1982](https://ubbern.swisscovery.slsp.ch/permalink/41SLSP_UBE/ar3q4e/alma99118009708505511), [Lindsay 1988](https://doi.org/10.18563/pv.18.2.95-154))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Miocene (Langhian), 15.98Ma\
**Minimum justification:** From the upper age of the locality (Burdigalian Marine stage)\
**Maximum age:** Early Miocene (Burdigalian), 18.3Ma\
**Maximum justification:** The oldest stem gerbils, represented by †_Myocricetodon_, are found in the same Siwalik deposits of the Potwar Plateau as †_Potwarmus_ ([Flynn et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-49753-8_15)), with the oldest dating as far back as 18.3Ma in the early Miocene ([Flynn et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-0182(94)00114-N), [Lazzari et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1671/0272-4634(2007)27[1062:FOIEOM]2.0.CO;2)). Consequently, Flynn et al. ([2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-49753-8_15)) suggest the murine/gerbil split was complete prior to ~16Ma.\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem murine, from a Miocene fluvial horizon in the Chinji Formation of Pakistan ([Jacobs and Flynn 2005](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789047416616_011)). Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)) considered †_Karnimata darwini_ (YGSP 7720) as a calibration for Murinae (_Mus_ + _Rattus_ split), whereas Aghová et al. ([2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2018.07.017)) use the same fossil to calibrate Crown Millardini/Otomyini/Arvicanthini and instead advocate †_Antemus chinjiensis_ for crown Murinae. Placement of †_Potwarmus_ varies in total-evidence analyses, sometimes occupying a position on the stem of Murinae + Deomyinae ([López-Antoñanzas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-024-02053-2)), but this topology conflicts with recent genomic estimates ([Upham et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000494), [Álvarez-Carretero et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04341-1)).

**Node 898:** Murinae (part: Murini + Arvicanthini) | 11.1–12.3Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Murini (_Mus musculus_) and Arvicanthini (_Arvicanthis niloticus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Karnimata_ sp. (YGSP 791)\
**Minimum age:** Middle Miocene (Tortonian), 11.1Ma\
**Minimum justification:** From the upper age of localities associated with the first appearance of the taxon ([Jacobs and Flynn 2005](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789047416616_011))\
**Maximum age:** Middle Miocene (Serravallian), 12.3Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Lower age of locality associated with appearance of the _Progonomys_ clade, which contains _Mus_ ([Jacobs 1978](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Fossil-rodents-(Rhizomyidae-and-Muridae)-:-from-Neogene-Siwalik-deposits-Pakistan/oclc/4611477), [Jacobs and Flynn 2005](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789047416616_011))\
**Discussion:** This taxon is the oldest-known stem member and basis for the naming of the †_Karnimata_ clade of Arvicanthini, Millardini, and Otomyini, which is sister to the †_Progonomys_ clade including Murini ([Kimura et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep14444), [López-Antoñanzas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-024-02053-2)). Placement of the taxon †_Progonomys_ itself varies in total-evidence analyses, sometimes occupying a position on the stem of Murinae ([López-Antoñanzas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-024-02053-2)), but this topology conflicts with recent genomic estimates ([Upham et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000494), [Álvarez-Carretero et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04341-1)). The age is based on the M1 specimens from localities Y791 and Y797 in Nagri Formation, Siwalik Group, Potwar Plateau, northern Pakistan ([Jacobs and Flynn 2005](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789047416616_011); [Kimura et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0069308), [2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep14444); [Aghová et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2018.07.017)).

**Node 827:** Carnivora | 37.3–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Caniformia (_Vulpes vulpes_) and Feliformia (_Lynx canadensis_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Hesperocyon gregarius_ (SMNH P1899.6) from the Lac Pelletier local fauna of the Cypress Hills Formation (Duchesnean NALMA), Saskatchewan, Canada ([Bryant 1992](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022336000020850))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Eocene (Bartonian), 37.3Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The oldest caniforms, including †_Hesperocyon_, are known from the early Duchesnean NALMA ([Robinson et al. 2004](http://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7312/wood13040.10)), which corresponds to the Bartonian\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** The earliest known stem carnivorans date to the early Paleocene ([Fox et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1666/09-165.1)), which we use to set the maximum at 66.04Ma ± 0.4 Myr, thus 66.0Ma\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem caniform from the middle Eocene (Bartonian) of Saskatchewan, Canada ([Wesley-Hunt and Flynn 2005](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1477201904001518), [Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))

**Node 791:** Artiodactyla | 52.4–66.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of Tylopoda (_Camelus dromedarius_) and Ruminantia (_Muntiacus reevesi_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Himalayacetus subathuensis_ (RUSB 2003) from the Subathu Formation (zone IIIc), India ([Bajpai and Gingerich 1998](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.95.26.15464))\
**Minimum age:** Early Eocene (Ypresian), 52.4Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The specimen was found in the Subathu Formation in India ([Bajpai and Gingerich 1998](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.95.26.15464)). As per Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)), it co-occurs with the foraminiferan †_Nummulites atacicus_, which is distributed across nanoplankton zones 11-12 ([Martín-Martín et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046%2Fj.0950-091x.2001.00158.x)), which permits setting the minimum age to 52.4Ma instead of using the top of the Ypresian stage (47.8Ma)\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Following Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)), we use the lack of Paleocene crown cetartiodactyls to suggest a plausible soft maximum age of 66.04 ± 0.4Ma, thus 66.0Ma\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem cetacean from the early Eocene (Ypresian) of India ([Bajpai and Gingerich 1998](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.95.26.15464), [O’Leary and Uhen 1999](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0094837300020376), [Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))

**Fossil taxa:** †_Heomys_ sp. (IVPP V4323) from the lower part of the Upper Member of the Wanghudun Formation, Anhui Province, China ([Li 1977](https://www.vertpala.ac.cn/EN/column/column1.shtml))\
