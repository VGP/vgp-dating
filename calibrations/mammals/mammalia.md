# Mammal calibrations

Our calibrations derive primarily from Upham et al. ([2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000494)) and Álvarez-Carretero et al. ([2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04341-1)), with recent updates and revisions therein.

### Calibrations:

**Node 783:** Mammalia | 201.5-164.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Most recent common ancestor of monotremes (_Ornithorhynchus anatinus_), marsupials (_Monodelphis domestica_), and placentals (_Dasypus novemcinctus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Ambondro mahabo_ (UA 10602) from the Isalo III Formation of the Mahajanga Basin in northwestern Madagascar ([Flynn et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/43420))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Jurassic (Bathonian), 164.9Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Specimen locality dated to Bathonian, top of which is 166.1 Ma, ± 1.2 Myr = 164.9 Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))\
**Maximum age:** Jurassic-Triassic Boundary, 201.5Ma\
**Maximum justification:** The closest known relative of crown mammals, †_Hadrocodium_, dates to early Jurassic Sinemurian ([Luo et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1058476)), and other closely related mammaliaformes are known from Late Triassic ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))\
**Discussion:** The oldest-known member of the monotreme clade Australosphenida from the middle Jurassic (Bathonian) of Madagascar ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424); [Flynn et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/43420); [Luo et al. 2002](https://www.app.pan.pl/article/pdf/41/item/app47-001.html); [Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291)), and also the oldest-known credible crown mammalian.

**Node 947:** Monotremata | 113.0-16.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Most recent common ancestor of _Tachyglossus aculeatus_ and _Ornithorhynchus anatinus_\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Obdurodon dicksoni_ (QMF20568) from the Riversleigh World Heritage Area in Queensland, Australia ([Archer et al. 1992](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/608888))\
**Minimum age:** Miocene, 15.97Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Oldest specimens found in northwestern Queensland in Faunal Zone B sites, which date to early Miocene ([Travouillon et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1080/03115510609506871))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous, 113.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Potential crown Monotremes are present in Argentina from ~61Ma, but the latest Australasian sites that lack any evidence of crown monotremes date to the Albian (Phillips 2015)\
**Discussion:** The oldest-known stem member of Ornithorhynchidae from the early Miocene (Burdigalian) of northwestern Queensland, Australia ([Phillips 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/490); [Luo et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05627); [Phillips et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0904649106)), and also the oldest-known crown monotrematan.

---

**Node 938:** Marsupialia | 131.3-47.6Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Most recent common ancestor of Paucituberculata (_Lestoros inca_), Didelphimorphia (_Monodelphis domestica_) and Australidelphia (_Dromiciops gliroides_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Djarthia murgonensis_ (QMF52748) from the Eocene Tingamarra Local Fauna at the Murgon fossil site in southeastern Queensland, Australia ([Godthelp et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1023%2FA%3A1020517808869))\
**Minimum age:** Eocene (Ypresian), 47.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Murgon locality is correlated with the Ypresian marine stage ([Beck 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-012-0953-x))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian), 131.3Ma\
**Maximum justification:**  Therians such as †_Sinodelphys szalayi_ date to the Barremian ([Luo et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1090718); [Zhou et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01420)) and are diverse, but evidently are not metatherian ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3); [Beck 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-08419-5_35)). Diversity of therians and possible eutherians during the Barremian implies a similar antiquity for Metatheria to at least that time, but such taxa pre-date the radiation of crown Marsupialia.\
**Discussion:** The oldest-known crown marsupial, from the early Eocene of Murgon, Australia ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424); Beck [2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-012-0953-x), [2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-08419-5_35)).

**Node 784:** Theria | 169.6-156.3Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of marsupials (_Monodelphis domestica_) and placentals (_Dasypus novemcinctus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Juramaia sinensis_ (PM1143) from the Daxigou site in the Middle–Late Jurassic Tiaojishan formation, Jianchang County, Liaoning Province, China ([Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291))\
**Minimum age:** Late Jurassic (Oxfordian), 156.3Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Radiometric dating sets the age of the Beipiao area at the Daxigou site at just over 160Ma, placing it within the Late Jurassic (Oxfordian), with a minimum bound of 157.3Ma ± 1.0 Myr, thus 156.3Ma ([Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291))\
**Maximum age:** Middle Jurassic (Bathonian), 169.6Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Following the rationale of Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)), we set the maximum age at 169.6Ma to account for viewpoints that place taxa such as †_Ambondro_ (see **Node 783:** Mammalia) as stem therians ([Woodburne et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1055-7903(03)00113-1)), constraining the therian maximum within the Jurassic (Bathonian) at 168.6Ma ± 1.0 Myr, thus 169.6Ma.\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem eutherian ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424); [Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10291)), and also the oldest-known crown therian.

**Node 785:** Placentalia | 126.0-61.6Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Atlantogenata (_Dasypus novemcinctus_) and Boroeutheria (_Tupaia tana_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Ravenictis krausei_ (UALVP 31175) from the Ravenscrag Formation of the early Puercan of Saskatchewan, Canada ([Fox & Youzwyshyn 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.1994.10011566))\
**Minimum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 61.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Ravenscrag Formation is from the early Puercan NALMA, corresponding to the Danian age of the Paleocene, the top of which corresponds to 61.7Ma ± 0.1 Myr, thus 61.6Ma ([Fox & Youzwyshyn 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.1994.10011566))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian/Aptian), ~126Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Maximum bound based on the Jehol Formation of the Early Cretaceous ([Zhou 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwu055), [Yang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1918272117)), a well-sampled fauna including stem placentals such as †_Ambolestes_ and †_Sinodelphys_ ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3)) but no putative crown members\
**Discussion:** This fossil is a stem carnivoran from the early Paleocene (Danian) of Ravenscrag Formation in Saskatchewan, Canada ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424); [Fox et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1666/09-165.1)), and therefore the oldest-known crown placentalian.

**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem proboscidean from the Paleocene (Selandian) of Morocco ([Gheerbrant et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089739)), and also the oldest-known crown atlantogenatan. Same constraint as **Node 931:** Afrotheria.

**Node 936:** Xenarthra | 126.0-47.6Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Cingulata (_Dasypus novemcinctus_) and Folivora (_Choloepus didactylus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Riostegotherium yanei_ (MCN-PV 1774) from the Itaboraí Formation of the Itaboraian land-mammal age (middle Paleocene) of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil ([Oliveira and Bergqvuist 1998](https://peapaleontologica.org.ar/index.php/peapa/article/view/174))\
**Minimum age:** Early Eocene (Ypresian), 47.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Itaboraí formation dates to the Ypresian of the early Eocene ([Woodburne et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10914-012-9222-1)), the top of which is 47.8Ma, ± 0.2 Myr, thus 47.6Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424))\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous (Barremian/Aptian), ~126Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Maximum bound based on the Jehol Formation of the Early Cretaceous ([Zhou 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwu055), [Yang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1918272117)), a well-sampled fauna including stem placentals such as †_Ambolestes_ and †_Sinodelphys_ ([Bi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0210-3)) but no putative crown members\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem cingulate from the early Eocene (Ypresian) of Itaboraí, Brazil ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424); [Bergqvist et al. 2004](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/en/periodiques/geodiversitas/26/2/les-xenarthra-mammalia-du-bassin-de-sao-jose-de-itaborai-paleocene-superieur-age-itaboraiense-rio-de-janeiro-bresil)), and also the oldest-known crown xenarthran.

**Node 845:** Chiroptera | 58.9-45.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Yinpterochiroptera (_Rousettus aegyptiacus_) and Yangochiroptera (_Rhynchonycteris naso_)\
**Fossil taxa:** †_Tanzanycteris mannardi_ (TNM MP-207) from the middle Eocene lake sediments of the Mahenge formation, Tanzania ([Gunnell et al. 2003](https://palaeo-electronica.org/2002_2/africa/issue2_02.htm))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Eocene (Lutetian), 45.0Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Age estimates using 206Pb/238U dating and sedimentary rate analysis put the lacustrine Mahenge formation at 45.83Ma ± 1.1 Myr and the fossil itself at a lower bound of 45.0Ma ([Harrison et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4615-1271-4_2))\
**Maximum age:** Late Paleocene (Thanetian), 58.9Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Following Phillips ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/490)), we use the base of the Thanetian as the upper bound to be conservative regarding stem bats that are from very close to the Thanetian-Ypresian boundary ([Ravel et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-011-0785-0)).\
**Discussion:** This fossil is the oldest-known stem member of Rhinolophoidea within Yinpterochiroptera, from the middle Eocene (Lutetian) of Tanzania in the Mahenge formation ([Phillips 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/490); [Flynn et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/43420); [Gunnell et al. 2003](https://palaeo-electronica.org/2002_2/africa/issue2_02.htm)). Jones et al. ([2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10914-024-09705-8)) identify the Ypresian †_Archaeonycterus praecursor_ as the oldest crown bat, but only for majority-rule consensus approaches, and only for trees not rooted on _Sorex_. The oldest stem bat in their analyses, †_Tachypteron,_ is dated to the Lutetian ([Lenz et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00531-014-1126-2)), supporting the minimum age of †_Tanzanycteris_.

**Node 915:** Lagomorpha | 66.0-47.6Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Leporidae (_Oryctolagus cuniculus_) and Ochotonidae (_Ochotona princeps_).\
**Fossil taxa:** Unnamed leporid fossils (GU/RSR/VAS 711) from the early Eocene Cambay Shales at the Vastan lignite mine, Gujarat, India ([Rose et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.1661))\
**Minimum age:** Early Eocene (Ypresian), 47.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The Cambay Shale are dated to the middle Ypresian ([Rose et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.1661)), the top of which dates to 47.8Ma ± 0.2 Myr, thus 47.6Ma\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Based on the occurrences of basal Glires, including †_Mimotona_ ([Asher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387); [de Vries & Beck, 2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249)) and †_Heomys_ ([Li & Ting, 1983](https://doi.org/10.5962/p.228600))\
**Discussion:** The oldest-known crown lagomorphs, these unnamed stem leporid fossils are from the Vastan Mines (Cambay Shale) of West-Central India, dated to the early Eocene (Ypresian) ([Benton et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424); [Rose et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.1661)). The oldest known stem Lagomorphs, †_Mimotona,_ have a concordant upper age of 66.0Ma ([Asher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387); [de Vries & Beck, 2023](https://doi.org/10.26879/1249)).

**Node 892:** Rodentia | 66.0-41.0Ma\
**Node calibrated:** The most recent common ancestor of Muroidea (_Mus musculus_), Sciuromorpha (_Sciurus vulgaris_), and Hystricomorpha (_Erethizon dorsatum_)\
**Fossil taxa:** _Ischyromys_ sp. (USNM PAL 617532) from West Canyon Creek, Wyoming, USA ([Heaton 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.019))\
**Minimum age:** Middle Eocene (Lutetian), 41.03Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The taxon is known from Late Eocene deposits at West Canyon Creek, Wyoming, dated to the Duchesnean NALMA ([Heaton 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.019)), and Hough Draw, Montana, dated to the late Uintan (Ui3) NALMA, corresponding to the Lutetian ([Tabrum et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.015))\
**Maximum age:** Early Paleocene (Danian), 66.0Ma\
**Maximum justification:** As for other nested, crown placental clades. **$XXX WHAT DOES THIS MEAN??**\
**Discussion:** †_Ischyromys_ sp. is a stem member of Sciuromorpha nested well within crown Rodentia ([Asher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387)), with the oldest specimens known from the Uintan (Ui3) NALMA (Lutetian) of Montana, USA (Hough Draw, [Tabrum et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511665431.015)). Older minimum ages for crown Rodentia would be justified if future studies sampling both extant and fossil species support the placements of taxa such as †_Tamquammys_ close to Ctenohystrica ([Marivaux & Boivin 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/zoolinnean/zlz048)) or †_Franimys_ close to Sciuridae ([Marivaux et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2004.00131.x)). Benton et al. ([2015](https://doi.org/10.26879/424)) advocated for using †_Paramys atavus_ (YPM-PU 14200) to calibrate crown Rodentia, citing several papers that placed †_Paramys_ in Sciuromorpha, within the crown group ([Korth 1984](https://doi.org/10.5962/p.228603); [McKenna and Bell 1997](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/classification-of-mammals/9780231110136/); [Marivaux et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2004.00131.x)). However, †_“Paramys” atavus_ is likely better assigned as †_Acritoparamys atavus_ ([Ivy 1990](https://hdl.handle.net/2027.42/48538)). More recent work that did not sample †_A. atavus_ but did code †_Paramys_ sp, †_P. delicatus,_ and †_P. copei,_ as well as †_Reithroparamys_ and older Asian Paleocene species such as †_Tribosphenomys,_ found that these taxa are stem rather than crown rodents ([Meng et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1206/0003-0090(2003)275%3C0001:TOORMG%3E2.0.CO;2); [Asher et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1107808); [2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.190387)). While Vianey-Laud and Marivaux ([2021](https://dx.doi.org/10.18563/pv.44.2.e2)) reconstruct †_Acritoparamys_ as falling within crown Rodentia, the analysis includes no extant taxa. We therefore suggest †_Ischyromys_ as a younger calibration for crown rodents.
