# Crocodylia calibrations

One terminal node (**731:** Crocodylia), separating _Alligator_ and _Gavialis_, generally dates to the Late/Upper Cretaceous, ~90Ma. While there is an extensive crocodilian fossil record – both stem and crown – dating back to the Triassic, there are only a few well-constrained fossils in the vicinity of Crocodylia based on recent phylogenetic analyses.

### Calibrations:

**Node 731:** Crocodylia | 113.2-77.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing all extant crocodylians.\
**Fossil taxa:** _Leidyosuchus canadensis_ (NMC 338, 1551)\
**Minimum age:** Late Cretaceous, at 77.9Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** The closest Type IV calibration is _Leidyosuchus canadensis_ [Lambe, 1907](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/10884799) (Cretaceous: Campanian, 77.9–72.1Ma) as an early crown-group member of Crocodylia (see [Brochu 1997](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02724634.1997.10011017)). This placement is supported by 8 total-evidence analyses (see below), generally on the stem of Alligatoroidea. We therefore place a minimum bound of 77.9Ma on this node.\
**Maximum age:** Early Cretaceous, 113.2Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** A possible maximum bound is the Aptian-Albian boundary (113.2Ma), as the lower 95% CI of both times and ages is 107.2Ma and 107.9Ma, respectively.\
**Discussion:** Other time-scaled parsimony analyses such as [Salas-Gismondi et al. 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14772019.2018.1495275) also support a Late Cretaceous origin for Crocodylia and a crown placement of _Leidyosuchus_, but do not report their exact age estimates and were therefore not considered.

### Likelihood checkpoints

TimeTree.org estimates a median age of 87Ma (72.9-101.0Ma) from 18 studies between 2004 and 2018. To this, we added 5 additional studies from 2006–2021. Excluding outliers (1.5 * IQR; in red, below), this yields a median of 87.4Ma with a range from 70.9–110.2Ma. The data are given in crocodylia_timetree.csv. To this, we can compare 8 estimates from 7 total-evidence studies with fossil tips from 2017–2025 to estimate a range of 88.3–107.9Ma (Median 97.4Ma) based on the average ages and CIs. This excludes two outlier ages, one from from Godoy et al. 2019 and the Groh et al. 2022 FBD analysis, which reports dramatically different topologies and timescales for Gavialoidea. The data are recorded in crocodylia_calibrations.csv.
