# Early vertebrate calibrations

We primarily focus here on the well-constrained ages discussed by [Benton et al. (2015)](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1) and [Carlisle et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adp7161).

### Calibrations:

**Node 585:** Vertebrata | 497.0–590.8Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing Cyclostomata and Gnathostomata.\
**Fossil taxon:** †_Furnishina bigeminata_ (GMPKU2589; Geological Museum, Peking University, Beijing, China).\
**Minimum age:** †_Furnishina_ is found in the middle Cambrian Huaqiao Formation at Wangcun Section, northern Hunan Province, China, dated to 497Ma ([Dong and Zhang 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-paleontology/article/middle-cambrian-through-lowermost-ordovician-conodonts-from-hunan-south-china/3DEF85E92A72769822D58F28473FDB5F)).\
**Minimum justification:** Conodonts are tooth-like ‘elements’ that have been interpreted as coming from vertebrates, possibly stem gnathostomes ([Donoghue and Keating 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/pala.12125)). Soft tissue fossils show notochords, segmental trunk musculature, a caudal fin, paired sensory organs, and gill pouches, all of which support a vertebrate affinity. The oldest conodont is †_Furnishina_, a paraconodont in the euconodont lineage ([Murdock et al. 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature12645)).\
**Maximum age:** Weng’an Biota, 590.8Ma ([Yang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abi9643)). Yang et al. date the biota based on Re-Os data to 587.2Ma ±3.6 Myr, yielding a maximum age constraint of 590.8Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** The Weng’an Biota yields exceptionally preserved macrofossils of algae but nothing that can definitively be placed into crown Metazoa. Embryo-like fossils preserved here have been suggested to be total group metazoans ([Yin et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2021.0032)), but this is still uncertain, and others have suggested they are non-metazoan holozoans ([Huldtgren, et al. 2011](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1209537)).\
**Discussion:** †_Arandaspis prionotolepis_ is a total-group gnathostome, making it crown-group Vertebrata, however it is dated to 457.5Ma and is therefore younger than †_Furnishina_ ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)). The Lantian Biota also has extensive macrofossils without any definitive metazoans, but it is older than the Weng’an Biota ([Yang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abi9643)), making the Weng’an a more reasonable choice.

**Node 1150:** Cyclostomata | 358.5–590.8Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing _Myxine glutinosa_ (Myxiniformes: Myxinidae) and _Lampetra fluviatilis_ (Petromyzontiformes: Petromyzontidae).\
**Fossil taxon:** †_Priscomyzon riniensis_ (AM5750, Albany Museum, Grahamstown, Eastern Cape, South Africa).\
**Minimum age:** The Witpoort Formation (Witteberg Group) at Waterloo Farm, Grahamstown, South Africa has been dated to the Famennian, and so we can use the Devonian-Carboniferous boundary of 358.9Ma ± 0.4 Myr, or 358.5Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1); [Becker et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-824360-2.00022-X)).\
**Maximum age:** Weng’an Biota, 590.8Ma ([Yang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abi9643)). Yang et al. date the biota based on Re-Os data to 587.2Ma ±3.6 Myr, yielding a maximum age constraint of 590.8Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** The unequivocally total-group petromyzontid (and therefore crown-group Cyclostomata) †_Priscomyzon riniensis_ is used as the minimum calibration ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1); [Gess et al. 2006](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05150); [Miyashita et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03305-9)).\
**Maximum justification:** The Weng’an Biota yields exceptionally preserved macrofossils of algae but nothing that can definitively be placed into crown Metazoa. Embryo-like fossils preserved here have been suggested to be total group metazoans ([Yin et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2021.0032)), but this is still uncertain, and others have suggested they are non-metazoan holozoans ([Huldtgren, et al. 2011](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1209537)).\
**Discussion:** The Lantian Biota (602Ma) also has extensive macrofossils without any definitive metazoans, but it is older than the Weng’an Biota (Yang et al. [2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abi9643), [2022](https://doi.org/10.1130/G49734.1)), making the Weng’an a more reasonable choice. One might also choose to set a much more recent maximum age of 506.5Ma based on the lower bound of the Miaolingian (Cambrian Series 3). Numerous fossil chordates, including stem-chordates (†_Pikaia_, †_Yunnanozoon_; [Mussini et al. 2023](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(24)00669-9)), stem or crown tunicates (†_Megasiphon_; [Nanglu et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39012-4)), and stem vertebrates (†Myllokunmingiidae; [Shu et al. 1999](https://www.nature.com/articles/46965)) are known from this stage, but no crown vertebrates or crown cyclostomes. However, this hypothesis does not allow for the possibility that †Euconodonta, which extend into the Cambrian, are placed on the stem of Cyclostomata as estimated in some recent analyses (Miyashita et al. [2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1814794116), [2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03305-9)). Consequently, we choose the more conservative maximum age of 590.8Ma to incorporate a potentially older Cambrian origin for Cyclostomata.

**Node 586:** Gnathostomata | 431.7–468.4Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing Osteichthyes and Chondrichthyes.\
**Fossil taxon:** †_Shenacanthus vermiformis_ (V300000; Institute of Vertebrate Palaeontology and Palaeoanthropology; [Zhu et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05136-8)).\
**Minimum age:** †_Shenacanthus_ is found in the early Silurian (Telychian) Huixingshao Formation of Chongqing, South China ([Zhu et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05136-8)). This has been established as Llandovery in age, and so a minimum age constraint can be established based on the Llandovery-Wenlock boundary. This has been dated to 432.9Ma ± 1.2 Myr ([Melchin et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-824360-2.00021-8)), yielding a minimum age of 431.7Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** †_Shenacanthus_ is resolved as a derived stem-chondrichthyan in a phylogenetic analysis by [Zhu et al. (2022)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05136-8).\
**Maximum age:** Ostracoderm assemblages from the Stairway Sandstone Formation of Mt Watt, Amadeus Basin, Northern Territory, Australia, dated to 467.3Ma ± 1.1 Myr, yielding a maximum age of 468.4Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)).\
**Maximum justification:** Ostracoderms are stem group gnathostomes, and their presence in the Ordovician demonstrates the potential for preservation of gnathostomes. However, no crown representatives have been found.\
**Discussion:** †_Guiyu oneiros_ has lobe-finned fish synapomorphies which identify it as a stem-sarcopterygian and therefore crown Osteichthyes and crown Gnathostomata, however, it is dated younger than †_Shenacanthus_ at 420.7Ma ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)). Scales from possible chondrichthyans have also been found from the Stairway Sandstone, but dated slightly older than †_Shenacanthus_ at 457.7Ma. However, these are difficult to place phylogenetically and this uncertainty makes them unsuitable for use as calibrations ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)). †_Nerepisacanthus_ is an acanthodian, making it crown gnathostome and possibly stem chondrichthyan ([Burrow and Rudkin 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0104171)). However, it is found in the Bertie Formation which is dated to 423Ma ± 2.3 Myr to 419.2Ma ± 3.2 Myr, making it either the same age or younger than †_Guiyu_, and much younger than †_Shenacanthus_.

**Node 587:** Osteichthyes | 420.7–444.9Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing Actinopterygii and Sarcopterygii.\
**Fossil taxon:** †_Guiyu oneiros_ (IVPP V15541, Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology, Beijing)\
**Minimum age:** †_Guiyu_ is found in the Kuanti Formation, Xiaoxiang Reservoir, Quijing, Yunnan, China. Based on conodont index fossils, the formation has been correlated with the Ludlow-Pridoli boundary, which has been dated to 423Ma ± 2.3 Myr, yielding a minimum age of 420.7Ma ([Melchin et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-824360-2.00021-8)).\
**Minimum justification:** †_Guiyu_ has lobe-finned fish synapomorphies which identify it as a stem sarcopterygian and therefore crown Osteichthyes ([Zhu et al. 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07855); [Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1)).\
**Maximum age:** The base of the Llandovery, dated at 443.4Ma ± 1.5 Myr, or 444.9Ma, can be used as the soft maximum calibration ([Melchin et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-824360-2.00021-8)).\
**Maximum justification:** There are records of diverse jawless fishes in the Silurian rocks, but nothing that can be identified as crown Osteichthyes ([Benton et al. 2015](http://palaeo-electronica.org/content/fc-1))
