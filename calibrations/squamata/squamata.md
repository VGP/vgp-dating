# Squamata calibrations

Squamates have a high density of well-preserved fossils, reviewed by [Simões and Pyron 2021](https://bioone.org/journals/bulletin-of-the-museum-of-comparative-zoology/volume-163/issue-2/0027-4100-163.2.47/THE-SQUAMATE-TREE-OF-LIFE/10.3099/0027-4100-163.2.47.short). Results from total-evidence analyses have been relatively consistent across most recent studies, providing a plethora of potential Type IV calibrations. In contrast, our limited sampling of squamates limits this applicability somewhat. Here, we primarily rely on the most recent total-evidence analysis of [Simões et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/advance-article/doi/10.1093/sysbio/syaf067/8266822) and references therein, using the nomenclature of [Burbrink et al. 2020](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/69/3/502/5573126) for node names. We can add another Type IV calibration from the recent time-calibrated morphological phylogenetic analyses analyses of [Benson et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09566-y). A secondary source of Type III calibrations is [Title et al. 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2449) (their Table S1) and references therein. A single Type III calibration for Chameleonidae is taken from [Čerňanský et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-57014-5). We place the maximum age of the Triassic (252.0Ma) as the maximum age of most constraints, consistent the earliest age for stem-group Squamata subtending _Megachirella_ from Simōes et al. 2026.


### Type IV Calibrations:
**Node 748:** Crown Squamata | 215-166Ma.\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing all extant squamates.\
**Fossil taxa:** _Eichstaettisaurus schroederi_ (BSPG 1937), _Helioscopus dickersonae_ (DINO 15914), _Eoscincus ornatus_ (DINO 14864), and _Paramacellodus sp._ (DINO 13861)\
**Minimum age:** Middle Jurassic, at 166Ma.\
**Minimum justification:** There has been substantial debate on the minimum age of crown squamates. Several fragmentary fossils have been labeled as “crown squamates” without testing this by including them in phylogenetic analyses ([Evans 1998](https://doi.org/10.1127/pala/250/1998/123)). Also, until recently no stem fossil squamate had been identified, making it harder to identify what fossils belong to the crown group. This situation has changed recently (e.g., [Simões et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0093-3/briefing/signup), [Tałanda et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05332-6)), and some fossils have been consistently placed within the squamate crown (as stem groups of crown squamate subclades), such as the extremely well-preserved stem gekkotan _Eichstaettisaurus schroederi_ ([Broili, 1938](https://catalog.nlm.nih.gov/permalink/01NLM_INST/m5fc0v/alma9917568313406676); see [Simões et al. 2017](https://academic.oup.com/zoolinnean/article-abstract/180/1/216/3799577)) from the Solnhofen Plattenkalk, in Bavaria, Germany, aged as Latest Kimmeridgian-early Tithonian (155-150Ma), Late Jurassic ([Schweigert 2007](https://www.schweizerbart.de/papers/njgpa/detail/245/59141/Ammonite_biostratigraphy_as_a_tool_for_dating_Uppe)), and the North American stem gekkotan _Helioscopus dickersonae_ and paramacellodid _Eoscincus ornatus_ from the Brushy Basin Member, Morrison Formation, Tithonian (150-149Ma), Late Jurassic ([Brownstein et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34217-5), [Meyer et al. 2023](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/290/2011/20232284/104268/A-Morrison-stem-gekkotan-reveals-gecko-evolution)). Additionally, more fragmentary fossils representing several species of paramacellodids — part of Total Group Scincoidea ([Bittencourt et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-020-0926-0), [Brownstein et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34217-5), [Gauthier et al. 2012](https://bioone.org/journals/bulletin-of-the-peabody-museum-of-natural-history/volume-53/issue-1/014.053.0101/Assembling-the-Squamate-Tree-of-Life--Perspectives-from-the/10.3374/014.053.0101.full), [Pyron 2017](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/66/1/38/2670090), [Reeder et al. 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118199), [Simões et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0093-3)) — come from the Middle Jurassic (~166Ma) of Scotland ([Evans and Waldman 1996](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/284761296_Small_reptiles_and_amphibians_from_the_Middle_Jurassic_of_Skye_Scotland), [Panciroli et al. 2020](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/link_gateway/2020EESTR.111..135P/doi:10.1017/S1755691020000055), [Waldman and Evans 1994](https://doi.org/10.1006/zjls.1994.1037)), and Kimmeridgian, Late Jurassic (157-152 ma) of Portugal and the US, including _Paramacellodus, Saurillus,_ and _Lisboasaurus_ ([Brownstein et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34217-5), [Evans 2003](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/biological-reviews/article/at-the-feet-of-the-dinosaurs-the-early-history-and-radiation-of-lizards/50B3C94F6F6F30D46FB909C9D84FECC6), [Meyer et al. 2023](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rspb.2023.2284), [Seiffert 1973](https://hdl.handle.net/2027/coo.31924004800219)). Snakes are also crown squamates that have been reported from Middle (~166Ma) and Late Jurassic sites in the UK, US, and Portugal ([Caldwell et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms6996), [LeBlanc et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-36422-2)).\
**Maximum age:** Late Triassic, 215 Ma.\
**Maximum justification:** Divergence time for crown Squamata has a range spanning between the Late Triassic and Early Jurassic among recent genomic and total evidence based studies: 193.2 Ma ([Burbrink et al. 2020](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/69/3/502/5573126)), 190.3 Ma ([Pyron 2017](https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/article/66/1/38/2670090)), 186.5 Ma ([Simões et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17190-9)), 214.5 Ma ([Simões et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syaf067)), and 213.2 Ma ([Title et al. 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.adh2449)). Therefore, we place a soft maximum age for the root reasonably older than the oldest estimate, at 215 Ma.\
**Discussion:** Divergence times for crown Squamata vary little between datasets and analyses, without any fossil evidence indicating crown squamates in the Triassic. A recent study suggested the presence of crown squamates in the Triassic based on the fossil taxon _Cryptovaranoides_ from the late Triassic of the UK ([Whiteside et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abq8274), [2024](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/11/11/231874/92497/Late-Triassic-Cryptovaranoides-microlanius-is-a)). However, several aspects of its anatomy and affinity to squamates have been subsequently corrected and phylogenetic interpretations altered ([Brownstein et al. 2023](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/10/10/230968/92232/The-affinities-of-the-Late-Triassic), [Caldwell et al. 2025](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107021), [Whiteside et al. 2024](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/11/11/231874/92497/Late-Triassic-Cryptovaranoides-microlanius-is-a)), raising doubt over its interpretation as a squamate, much less a crown squamate ([Brownstein et al. 2023](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/10/10/230968/92232/The-affinities-of-the-Late-Triassic), [Caldwell et al. 2025](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107021)). We therefore also consider this taxon unfit for node calibration.\

**767:** Toxicofera: 167.0Ma. This calibration is based on the earliest potential crown toxicoferan _Breugnathair elgolensis_ (Parviraptoridae), from the Bathonian of the middle Jurassic (165.3–168.2Ma) analyzed by Benson et al. 2025 (their Datasets 2 and 3). **This placement has not been corroborated by other total-evidence analyses, but the timescale is highly congruent with most recent estimates, including Title et al. 2024 and Simões et al. 2026.**

**751:** Laterata: 130.2Ma. This calibration is based on the earliest stem Teiiodea taxon _Dalinghosaurus longidigitus_ , from the Barremian of the early Cretaceous (128.0–132.0Ma) analyzed by Simões et al. 2026.


### Type III Calibrations:
**782:** Gekkota: 97.0Ma. This calibration is based on _Cretaceogekko burmae_, the oldest crown gekkotan from the Cenomanian of the Cretaceous (93.9–100.5Ma).

**749:** Unidentata: 170.0Ma. This calibration is based on the earliest Paramacellodidae (stem Scincoidea) material, from the Bajocian of the Jurassic (168.2–170.9Ma).

**779:** Anguimorpha: 144.0Ma. This calibration is based on the oldest crown anguimorph _Dorsetisaurus purbeckensis_, from the Tithonian of the Jurassic (143.1–149.2Ma).

**780:** Stem Anguidae (_Anniella + Elgaria_): 33.0Ma. This calibration is based on _Ophisaurus_ sp., the oldest crown anguine from the early Oligocene (23.0–33.9Ma), parameterizing the Anniellidae + Anguidae divergence (i.e., as a stem anguid).

**777:** Tropical Pleurodonta (_Anolis + Iguana_): 49.1Ma. This calibration is based on _Afairiguana avius_, the oldest stem polychrotid from the early Eocene (48.5–53.5Ma), parameterizing the Dactyloidae + Iguanidae divergence which encompasses Polychrotidae.  

**778:** Chamaeleonidae: 20.0Ma. This calibration is based on the oldest crown chameleonid _Calumma benovskyi_, from the early Miocene at the base of the Hiwegi formation, dated to ~17–20Ma ([Maxbauer et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.26879/342)). Assignment of this material to _Calumma_ places it within the _Bradypodion + Furcifer_ divergence, which has previously been estimated at ~45–50Ma using molecular timetrees ([Tolley et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.0184)).

**774:** Stem Boidae: 58.0Ma. This calibration is based on _Titanoboa cerrejonensis_, the oldest stem Boinae from the middle-late Paleocene (58.0–60.0Ma), parameterizing the Boidae + Pythonidae divergence (i.e., as a stem boid).

**769:** Endoglyptodonta: 20.0Ma. This calibration is based on _Vipera_ cf. _aspis_, the oldest crown Viperinae from the early Miocene (16.0–20.5Ma), parameterizing the Viperidae + Colubroides divergence (i.e., as a stem viperid).

**771:** Natricidae: 13.8Ma. This calibration is based on _Natrix_ aff. _longivertebrata_, the oldest crown Natricinae from the middle Miocene (11.6–16.0Ma), parameterizing the _Natrix + Thamnophis_ divergence based on its closer affinity to the European genus _Natrix_ than New World _Thamnophis_.

### Likelihood checkpoints

A preliminary literature review yields 44 age estimates for crown-group Squamata from 31 studies, yielding a median of 198.8Ma and a kernel-density mode of 203.0Ma. **These need to be expanded and filtered for inclusion of Dibamia**
