# Bird calibrations

Our bird calibrations are primarily adapted from Stiller et al. ([2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07323-1)).


### Calibrations:

**Node 709:** Galloanserae | 92.73–65.65Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of the clade encompassing Galliformes (_Gallus gallus_) and Anseriformes (_Anser anser_)\
**Fossil taxa:** Numerous fossil taxa from multiple continents satisfying the criteria of Parham et al. ([2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syr107)) support this calibration. These are listed below with references justifying the synapomorphies or phylogenetic analyses allying them with the crown of Galloanserae:

- _Conflicto antarcticus_ Tambussi, Degrange, de Mendoza, Sferco, & Santillana, 2019 – a nearly complete skeleton (MLP 07-III-1-1) from Level 10 of the López de Bertodano Formation at Seymour Island, Antarctica. A cladistic analysis including a variety of extant and extinct galloanserines placed the holotype of Conflicto antarticus as a stem Anseriformes ([Tambussi et al. 2019](https://academic.oup.com/zoolinnean/article-abstract/186/3/673/5281199)), a result further supported by subsequent cladistic and Bayesian analyses ([Field et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2096-0), [Musser & Clarke 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0278737)).

- _Presbyornis mongoliensis_ Kurochkin & Dyke, 2009 – tarsometatarsals (PIN 3104-6, 3104-24) from the Naran and Zhygden members of Tsagaan Khushuu, southern Gobi Desert, Ömnögovi Aimag, Mongolia (Paleocene, 58.7–55.8Ma), Mongolia ([Kurochkin & Dyke 2009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/gj.1177)).

- _Anachronornis anhimops_ Houde, Dickson, & Camerena, 2023 – nearly complete skeleton including the skull (USNM 496700) from site T56N R101W, Clark Quadrangle, Park County, Wyoming. Dated as latest Thanetian (56.22–55.80Ma). Phylogenetic analyses recovered _Anachronornis_ as a stem Anseriformes ([Houde et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-2818/15/2/233)).

- _Nettapterornis oxfordi_ ([Olson 1999](https://repository.si.edu/items/a9f15a68-b2e8-45c5-92ef-cf4ed854a469)) – articulated partial skeleton (BMNH Paleo A5922) from London Clay at Walton-on-the-Naze (early Ypresian, 54.6–55Ma), United Kingdom. Phylogenetic analyses place this taxon at either the stem or the crown of Anseriformes ([Mayr & Kitchener 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12542-025-00730-3)).

- Unnamed anseriform – partial quadrate (QM F23019) from the Tingamarra Local Fauna (early Eocene, 54.65–54.55Ma) of Murgon, Queensland, Australia ([Elzanowski & Boles 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-4983.2012.01166.x)).

- _Namaortyx sperrgebietensis_ Mourer-Chauviré, Pickford, & Senut, 2011 – almost complete tarsometatarsus (GSN No. SS 8’08) from the Middle Eocene (49–47Ma) of Namibia ([Mourer-Chauviré et al. 2011](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/link_gateway/2011JOrni.152..617M/doi:10.1007/s10336-010-0630-9)).

- _Chaunoides antiquus_ [Alvarenga, 1999](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/item/266361#page/233/mode/1up) – associated bones of at least three individuals (MNRJ 4619-V to 4632-V) from montmorillonite clays of the Tremembé Formation at Fazenda Santa Fé, Sao Paulo, Brazil (late Oligocene to early Miocene, 27.3–16Ma). Phylogenetic analysis confirmed this fossil as a stem Anhimidae ([Musser & Clarke 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0278737)).

- _Manuherikia lacustrina_ Worthy, Tennyson, Jones, McNamara, & Douglas, 2007 – isolated long bones (MNZ S40094, S42306-8) from the Bannockburn Formation, St Bathans Fauna (Antonian, 19–16Ma), Otago, New Zealand ([Worthy et al. 2007](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1017/S1477201906001957), [Worthy & Lee 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-4983.2008.00778.x)).

**Minimum age:** 65.65Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The oldest of these taxa, _Conflicto antarcticus_, is from Level 10 of the López de Bertodano Formation, which is the youngest stratum of the formation and sits right above the K-Pg boundary (66.04Ma); its upper bound was estimated as 65.65Ma based on dinoflagellate cyst biostratigraphy and U–Pb zircon dating ([Bowman et al. 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1342937X15002828)).\
**Maximum age:** 91.91Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Based on the 95% quantile of the empirical calibration distribution using first fossil occurrences of Galloanseres on each continent and the CladeDate algorithm ([Claramunt 2022](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/2041-210X.13977)).\
**Discussion:** While we simplify the CladeDate results for boundaries that are compatible with mcmctree and treePL, the original calibration function results in a skewStudent distribution with xi=65.73, omega=6.415, alpha=4.680e+07, and nu=2.143.

**Node 710:** Anatidae | 38–24Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of Anatinae (_Anas platyrhynchos)_ and Anserinae (_Anser anser_)\
**Fossil taxa:** Numerous fossil taxa from multiple continents satisfying the criteria of Parham et al. ([2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syr107)) support this calibration. These are listed below with references justifying the synapomorphies or phylogenetic analyses allying them with the crown of Anatidae:

- _Pinpanetta tedfordi_ Worthy, 2009 – complete right humerus (SAMA P.41257) from the Etadunna Formation at Young Bucks Quarry, Lake Palankarinna, South Australia, Australia. This locality is shared with three other contemporaneous fossil ducks: _P. vickersrichae_ and _P. fromensis,_ and _Australotadorna alecwilsoni_ ([Worthy 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2008.00483.x)). A cladistic analysis of _Pinpanetta_ (including _P. tedfordi_) placed it as a stem member of Erismaturinae ([Worthy 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1096-3642.2008.00483.x)).

- _Mionetta blanchardi_ ([Milne-Edwards, 1863](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/3679973#page/1224/mode/1up)) – multiple bones from several individuals including UCBL-FSL 442.807 and 442.800 from Saint-Gérand-le-Puy (Aquitanian, 23–20.4 Ma), Allier, France ([Worthy & Lee 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-4983.2008.00778.x)).

- _Mionetta sp._ – distal humerus (GSN GT 133’06) from Grillental (early Miocene, 20 Ma), Northern Sperrgebiet, Namibia ([Mourer-Chauviré 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/285787143_Birds_Aves_from_the_Early_Miocene_of_the_Northern_Sperrgebiet_Namibia)).

- _Manuherikia lacustrina_ Worthy, Tennyson, Jones, McNamara, & Douglas, 2007 – isolated long bones (MNZ S40094, S42306-8) from the Bannockburn Formation, St Bathans Fauna (Antonian, 19–16Ma), Otago, New Zealand ([Worthy et al. 2007](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1017/S1477201906001957), [Worthy & Lee 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-4983.2008.00778.x)).

- Bone fragments representing multiple anatid genera are known from Sharga (Middle Miocene, NMU7, MN7–8, 13.1–11.2 Ma), Shargyn-Govi area, Mongolia ([Zelenkov 2011]([url](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0031030111020195))). These include _Sharganetta mongolica_ Zelenkov, 2011 (PIN 4869/195 and 4869/198), _Nogusunna conflictoides_ Zelenkov, 2011 (PIN 4869/23, 4869/197, and 4869/199), _Protomelanitta gracilis_ Zelenkov, 2011 (PIN 4869/100, 4869/125, 4869/134, 4869/151), and _Chenoanas deserta_ [Zelenkov, 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0031030112050139) (PIN 4869/57, 4869/196). 

- _Megalodytes morejohni_ Howard, 1992 – long bones (LACM 123991–3, 123995) from the Round Mountain Member of the Temblor Formation (Langhian, 15.99–11.63Ma), Kern County, California, USA ([Howard 1992](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/item/348547#page/273/mode/1up)). 

- _Chloephaga robusta_ Tambussi, 1998 – distal ulna (MLP 57-VIl-23-47) from the Irene Formation (early Pliocene, 4–3Ma), Buenos Aires, Argentina ([Tambussi 1998](https://raco.cat/index.php/BolletiSHNBalears/article/view/169326)). There are many other fossils from this locality referred to Anatidae, but they most likely represent stem taxa.

- _Alopochen sirabensis_ ([Andrews, 1897](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1474-919X.1897.tb03281.x)) – bone fragments (UA 360) from the Pleistocene of Ampasambazimba (0.0229–0.0222 Ma), Madagascar ([Goodman 1999](https://www.internationalornithology.org/PROCEEDINGS_Durban/Symposium/S50/S50.4.htm)).

**Minimum age:** 24Ma\
**Minimum justification:** Given the uncertainty in the relationships among anatid subfamilies, we use _Pinpanetta_ to calibrate crown Anatidae. Biostratigraphy of fossil land mammals, presence of Oligocene foraminiferal fauna, and magnetostratigraphy indicate an age between 24 and 26Ma for the Etadunna Formation ([Woodburne et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.1994.10011527)).\
**Maximum age:** 38.05Ma\
**Maximum justification:** Based on the 95% quantile of the empirical calibration distribution using first fossil occurrences of Anatidae on each continent and the CladeDate algorithm ([Claramunt 2022](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/2041-210X.13977)).\
**Discussion:** While we simplify the CladeDate results for boundaries that are compatible with mcmctree and treePL, the original calibration function results in a skewStudent distribution with xi=24.701, omega=3.477, alpha=10.192, and nu=2.627.

**Node 699:** Otidimorphae | 80.39–51.81Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of Otidiformes (_Chlamydotis macqueenii_), Musophagiformes (_Tauraco erythrolophus_), and Cuculiformes (_Cuculus canorus_), sensu Braun et al. ([2019](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-16477-5_6))\
**Fossil taxa:** Numerous fossil taxa from multiple continents satisfying the criteria of Parham et al. ([2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syr107)) support this calibration. These are listed below with references justifying the synapomorphies or phylogenetic analyses allying them with the crown of Otidimorphae:

- _Foro panarium_ [Olson, 1992](http://hdl.handle.net/10088/8418) – nearly complete skeleton (USNM 336621) from the Fossil Butte Member (52.13–51.81 Ma) of the Green River Formation at Thompson Quarry, Lincoln County, Wyoming, USA. Phylogenetic analysis placed this taxon in the stem of Musophagiformes ([Field & Hsiang 2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-018-1212-3)). 

- _Chambicuculus pusillus_ [Mourer-Chauviré, Tabuce, Essid, Marivaux, Khayati, Vianey-Liaud, & Ben Haj Ali, 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/325753653_A_small_galliform_and_a_small_cuculiform_from_the_Eocene_of_Tunisia) – represented by fragments of multiple bones (ONM CB1-541, CB1-560–4, CB2-1001) from the Eocene (51–44.6 Ma, [Coster et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1130/B30565.1)) of Djebel Chambi, Tunisia ([Mourer-Chauviré et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.18563/pv.40.1.e2)).

- _Eocuculus_ cf. _cherpinae_ [Chandler, 1999](https://www.npshistory.com/publications/paleontology/grdtr-99-03-4.pdf) – partial postcranial skeleton (SMF Av 425) from the Early Oligocene (MP24, 30–28.8 Ma) of Pichovet, France ([Mayr 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geobios.2005.10.007)).

- Otididae indet. from the Oligocene (33.9–23.0 Ma) of Tchelkar Nura, Kazakhstan ([Kurochkin 1976](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/part/352126)). This material is not referenced to the specimen level, nor are explicit synapomorphies or phylogenetic analyses provided, but it is included here by reference for the sake of completeness.

- _Centropus bairdi_ [Shute, Prideaux, & Worthy, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/zoj.12387) – long bones (WAM 09.3.277–282) from Leaena’s Breath Cave, Nullarbor Plain, Western, Australia ([Shute et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/zoj.12387)). Magnetostratigraphy and faunistic correlates suggest an early Pleistocene age within the Naracoortean land mammal age (2.58-0.78Ma, [Shute et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/zoj.12387)).

- _Crotophaga ani_ [Linnaeus, 1758](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/542) – humeri and partial mandible, likely uncatalogued in the Natural History Museum of Denmark (formerly the University of Copenhagen Zoological Museum), from Lapa da Escrivania level 5, Lagoa Santa, Minas Gerais, Brazil ([Winge 1887](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/13380), [Nascimento & Silveira 2020](https://doi.org/10.11646/zootaxa.4743.4.2)). This formation contains remains deposited during the Pleistocene-Holocene transition, about 0.012–0.010Ma ([Nascimento & Silveira 2020](https://doi.org/10.11646/zootaxa.4743.4.2)).

- Various fragmentary remains (DLC 17268, 18041, 18098, 18157, 18780, 18797, 18876) of five species of _Coua_ (Cuculidae) from Ankilitelo Cave (0.013–0.002Ma) Madagascar ([Goodman et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.11646/zootaxa.3750.5.6)). 

**Minimum age:** 51.81Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The oldest of these taxa is _Foro panarium_, for which radiometric 40Ar/39Ar-dating of the K-spar tuff near the top of the Fossil Butte member yielded a recalibrated date of 51.97±0.16 Ma (Smith et al. 2008, 2010).
**Maximum age:** 80.39Ma\
**Maximum justification:** based on the 95% quantile of the empirical calibration distribution using first fossil occurrences of Anatidae on each continent and the CladeDate algorithm ([Claramunt 2022](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/2041-210X.13977)). While we simplify the CladeDate results for boundaries that are compatible with mcmctree and treePL, the original calibration function results in a skewStudent distribution with xi=51.91, omega=7.378, alpha=6.311e+05, and nu=2.763.\
**Discussion:** Relationships among Otidiformes, Musophagiformes and Cuculiformes are poorly resolved due to a very short internode and high levels of incomplete lineage sorting ([Stiller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07323-1)). We applied this calibration based on _Foro panarium_ (Musophagiformes _incertae sedis_) to the entire crown group, noting that both it and _Eocuculus_ have been very difficult to place phylogenetically – support for a position within Musophagiformes and Otidimorphae, respectively, are ambiguous across analyses.

**Node 704:** Daedalornithes | 95.7-54.6Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of Aegothelidae (_Aegotheles albertisi_) and Apodiformes (_Apus apus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** Numerous fossil taxa from multiple continents satisfying the criteria of Parham et al. ([2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syr107)) support this calibration. These are listed below with references justifying the synapomorphies or phylogenetic analyses allying them with the crown of Daedalornithes:

-  _Eocypselus vincenti_ [Harrison 1984](https://natuurtijdschriften.nl/pub/521070) – referred material, a nearly complete skeleton (MGUH 29278) and two incomplete postcranial skeletons (MGUH 26729, MGUH 26730) from the early Eocene (55.8–54.6 Ma) Fur Formation, Denmark. A cladistic analysis ([Ksepka et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.0580)) and Bayesian analyses with topological constraints ([Chen et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11090143)) found _E. vincenti_ on the stem of Apodiformes, but a total evidence Bayesian analysis indicated a position on the stem of Aegothelidae instead ([Chen et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11090143)). Either way, _Eocypselus_ is confirmed as a basal crown Daedalornithes.

- _Eocypselus rowei_ [Ksepka, Kulp, Nesbitt, Clarke, & Grande, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.0580) – a complete skeleton (WDC CGR-109) from the Fossil Butte Member of the Green River Formation (Early Eocene, 51.97±0.16 Ma), Wyoming, USA ([Ksepka et. al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.0580)).         

- _Collocalia buday_ [Boles, 2001](https://publications.australian.museum/a-swiftlet-apodidae-collocaliini-from-the-miocene-of-riversleigh-northwestern-queensland/) – left humerus (QM F20907) from the early Miocene Camel Sputum Site (18.53–16.97 Ma, [Woodhead et al. 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1342937X14003086)), Riversleigh, Queensland, Australia (see [Worthy & Nguyen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/03721426.2020.1756560)).

- _Aegotheles zealandivetus_ [Worthy, Scofield, Salisbury, Hand, De Pietri, & Archer, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10336-022-01981-6) – various material (CM 2013.18.1127; NMNZ S.42800, S.52761, S.52917) from the Bannockburn Formation, St Bathans Fauna (Early Miocene, Altonian, 18.26–15.97Ma), Otago, New Zealand ([Worthy et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10336-022-01981-6)).

- _Tachymarptis_ sp. – a single humerus, currently undescribed, first reported by Rich ([1980](https://hdl.handle.net/10520/AJA00382353_1817)) from the upper Varswater Formation at Langebaanweg (Early Pliocene 5.33–3.6 Ma), Cape Province, South Africa ([Manegold et al. 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259272396_The_Early_Pliocene_avifauna_of_Langebaanweg_South_Africa_a_review_and_update)).

- _Apus_ sp. – material representing a left coracoid (NSMT PV 24548), left carpometacarpus (24549), and proximal phalanx major digit (24550) from fissure-filling deposits in Shiriya, Aomori Prefecture, Shiriya, Aomori Prefecture, Japan; Late Pleistocene (~MIS 5e: 0.129–0.116Ma, [Watanabe et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.4202/app.00509.2018)).

- _Streptoprocne zonaris_ ([Shaw, 1796](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_cimelia-physica-figures_shaw-george_1796)) – unspecified, possibly uncatalogued specimen from Barra do Antonião, Piauí, Brazil (Late Pleistocene: 0.129–0.0117Ma, [Guérin et al. 1996](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291774116_A_fauna_pleistocenica_do_Piaui_Nordeste_do_Brasil_Relacoes_paleoecologicas_e_biocronologicas)).

**Minimum age:** 54.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The oldest of these fossils is _Eocypselus vincenti_, for which the bottom of the Fur Formation was deposited during the recovery phase of the Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum around 55.8 Ma ago (Stokke et al. 2020a). Basalts at the top of the Formation correlate with “subphase 2b” of pyroclastic deposition in the Balder Formation, suggesting a minimum age of 54.6 Ma (King 2016, Stokke et al. 2020b).\
**Maximum age:** 83.85 Ma\
**Maximum justification:** based on the 95% quantile of the empirical calibration distribution using first fossil occurrences of Anatidae on each continent and the CladeDate algorithm ([Claramunt 2022](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/2041-210X.13977)). While we simplify the CladeDate results for boundaries that are compatible with mcmctree and treePL, the original calibration function results in a skewStudent distribution with xi=54.649, omega=7.722, alpha=37.041, and nu=2.233.\
**Discussion:** Based on Stiller et al. ([2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07323-1)).

**Node 708:** Apodi | 103.40-50.3Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of Apodidae (_Apus apus_) and Hemiprocnidae (_Hemiprocne comata_)
**Fossil taxa:** Numerous fossil taxa from multiple continents satisfying the criteria of Parham et al. ([2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syr107)) support this calibration. These are listed below with references justifying the synapomorphies or phylogenetic analyses allying them with the crown of Apodi:

- _Scaniacypselus wardi_ [Harrison, 1984](https://natuurtijdschriften.nl/pub/521070) – articulated pectoral girdle and forelimbs (BMNH A5430) from Bed R6 (50.3–51.5 Ma) of the Røsnæs Clay Formation in Ølst, Denmark. Cladistic analyses ([Mayr 2003](https://doi.org/10.1642/0004-8038(2003)120[0145:POETSA]2.0.CO;2), [Ksepka et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.0580)) and Bayesian analyses with topological constraints ([Chen et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11090143)) placed it on the stem of Apodidae.

- _Collocalia buday_ [Boles, 2001](https://publications.australian.museum/a-swiftlet-apodidae-collocaliini-from-the-miocene-of-riversleigh-northwestern-queensland/) – left humerus (QM F20907) from the early Miocene Camel Sputum Site (18.53–16.97 Ma, [Woodhead et al. 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1342937X14003086)), Riversleigh, Queensland, Australia (see [Worthy & Nguyen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/03721426.2020.1756560)).

- _Collocalia_ sp. from the Bannockburn Formation, St Bathans Fauna (Early Miocene, Altonian, 18.26–15.97 Ma), Otago, New Zealand ([Worthy et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1477201906001957)). Although not identified at the species level, it is estimated to be much smaller than Collocalia buday ([Worthy et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1477201906001957)).
         
- _Tachymarptis_ sp. – a single humerus, currently undescribed, first reported by Rich ([1980](https://hdl.handle.net/10520/AJA00382353_1817)) from the upper Varswater Formation at Langebaanweg (Early Pliocene 5.33–3.6 Ma), Cape Province, South Africa ([Manegold et al. 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259272396_The_Early_Pliocene_avifauna_of_Langebaanweg_South_Africa_a_review_and_update)).

- _Apus_ sp. – material representing a left coracoid (NSMT PV 24548), left carpometacarpus (24549), and proximal phalanx major digit (24550) from fissure-filling deposits in Shiriya, Aomori Prefecture, Shiriya, Aomori Prefecture, Japan; Late Pleistocene (~MIS 5e: 0.129–0.116Ma, [Watanabe et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.4202/app.00509.2018)).
  
- _Streptoprocne zonaris_ ([Shaw, 1796](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_cimelia-physica-figures_shaw-george_1796)) – unspecified, possibly uncatalogued specimen from Barra do Antonião, Piauí, Brazil (Late Pleistocene: 0.129–0.0117Ma, [Guérin et al. 1996](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291774116_A_fauna_pleistocenica_do_Piaui_Nordeste_do_Brasil_Relacoes_paleoecologicas_e_biocronologicas)).

- _Chaetura pelagica_ ([Linnaeus, 1758](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/542)) from the Late Pleistocene (0.1–0.0117 Ma) of Clark's Cave, Bath County, Virginia, USA ([Guilday et al. 1977](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/part/228581)).

**Minimum age:** 50.3Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The oldest fossil is _Scaniacypselus wardi,_ for which the top of Member R6 is overlaid conformably by the Lillebælt Clay Formation; its base coincides with a sharp transition from calcareous to non-calcareous foraminifera, including _Reticulophragmium amplectens,_ which correlates with the NS18-NS19 boundary at around 50.3 Ma ([King 2016](https://doi.org/10.1144/SR27), fig. 76). The lowest part of R6 (or the top of the underlying Member R5) shows the last occurrence of the dinoflagellate cysts _Ochetodinium romanum,_ which represent the top of Subzone DE8b ([King 2016](https://doi.org/10.1144/SR27)) at around 51.5 Ma ([Speijer et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-824360-2.00028-0)). King ([2016](https://doi.org/10.1144/SR27)) shows a very narrow Subzone DE8b containing Ochetodinium romanum and right after the polarity reversal in the middle of C23, which is 51.72 Ma ([Speijer et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-824360-2.00028-0)).\
**Maximum age:** 103.40 Ma\
**Maximum justification:** based on the 95% quantile of the empirical calibration distribution using first fossil occurrences of Apodi on each continent and the CladeDate algorithm ([Claramunt 2022](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/2041-210X.13977)). While we simplify the CladeDate results for boundaries that are compatible with mcmctree and treePL, the original calibration function results in a skewStudent distribution with xi=50.32, omega=11.39, alpha=61.85, and nu=1.90.\
**Discussion:** Based on Stiller et al. ([2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07323-1)).

**Node 675:** Gruiformes | 80.4-55.6Ma\
**Node calibrated:** Last common ancestor of Gruoidea (_Grus grus_) and Ralloidea (_Gallinula chloropus_)\
**Fossil taxa:** Numerous fossil taxa from multiple continents satisfying the criteria of Parham et al. ([2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syr107)) support this calibration. These are listed below with references justifying the synapomorphies or phylogenetic analyses allying them with the crown of Gruiformes:

- _Pellornis mikkelseni_ [Bertelli, Chiappe, & Mayr, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/14772019.2010.538730) – a nearly complete articulated skeleton (holotype MGUH 29278) from the Fur Formation near Sundby, Isle of Mors, Jutland, Denmark ([Hoch 1997](https://2dgf.dk/dgf-online-series/notes-on-palaeornithology-and-on-a-new-bird-from-the-early-tertiary-north-sea-region/), [Musser et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11070102)). Initial cladistic analyses indicated a position of Messelornithidae, including Pellornis mikkelseni, as stem Rallidae ([Bertelli et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/14772019.2010.538730)). More recent analyses including additional characters from a more complete preparation of the holotype revealing a complete skull among other parts, indicated a more basal position as stem Ralloidea ([Musser et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11070102), [Musser & Clarke 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2020.559929)), thus setting a minimum age for crown Gruiformes.

- _Songzia acutunguis_ [Hou, 1990](https://www.vertpala.ac.cn/EN/volumn/volumn_134.shtml#1) – articulated skeletons (IVPP 18187–8) from the Yangxi Formation (55.8–48.6Ma), China ([Wang et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/03115518.2012.673302)), phylogenetically placed in Messelornithidae by cladistic analysis ([Musser et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11070102)).

- _Messelornis nearctica_ [Hesse, 1992](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/item/348547#page/213/mode/1up) – several skeletons (BHI 1283, 1292, 1294; FMNH A 716; SMF Av 406; SMNK-PAL 3810; USNM 336269, 336277, 776273, 776275) from the Fossil Butte Member of the Green River Formation (51.97±0.16Ma), Wyoming, USA ([Weidig 2010](https://journals.australian.museum/weidig-2010-rec-aust-mus-621-2944/)). See Musser et al. ([2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11070102)) for phylogenetic analysis.

- Rallidae indet. – distal end of left tarsometatarsus (DPC 3858) from the upper sequence of Quarry M in the Jebel Qatrani Formation (early Oligocene, 33.9–28.4 Ma) at Fayum, Egypt ([Rasmussen et al. 1987](http://dx.doi.org/10.5479/si.00810266.62.1)).

- _Australlus disneyi_ ([Boles, 2005](https://journals.australian.museum/boles-2005-rec-aust-mus-572-179190/)), specimens (QM F20906, F23723, F31469–76) from the White Hunter Site (Faunal Zone A, late Oligocene, 26–23.04Ma), Riversleigh, Queensland, Australia. - _?Aptornis proasciarostratus_ [Worthy, Tennyson, & Scofield, 2011b](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10336-011-0649-6) – thoracic vertebrae (NMNZ S.52350, S.52353) from the Bannockburn Formation (early Miocene, Altonian, 18.26–15.97Ma) of Otago, New Zealand ([Worthy et al. 2011b](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10336-011-0649-6)). Although the Miocene fossils are fragmentary, the analysis of Quaternary fossils indicated that Aptornis belongs in crown Gruiformes, either in the Ralloidea ([Boast et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/d11020024)) or sister to Psophiidae ([Musser & Cracraft 2019](https://doi.org/10.1206/3927.1)).

- Fragmentary remains of _Gallinula chloropus_ ([Linnaeus, 1758](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/542)), _Porphyrio porphyrio_ ([Linnaeus, 1758](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/542)), and _Hovacrex roberti_ ([Andrews, 1897](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1474-919X.1897.tb03281.x)) from Antsirabe (Holocene, 0.002–0 Ma), Madagascar reported by Goodman ([1999](https://www.internationalornithology.org/PROCEEDINGS_Durban/Symposium/S50/S50.4.htm)) from the BMNH and UA collections (catalog numbers unspecified) and references therein.

**Minimum age:** 55.6Ma\
**Minimum justification:** The oldest of these fossils is _Pellornis mikkelseni,_ for which MGUH 29278 is preserved in silicified diatomite, and thus must derive from one of two horizons near ash layers -19 or -21 in the lower part of the Knudeklint Member.
